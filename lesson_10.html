Module 3 • Lesson 3💡 Dodge & Burn: Sculpting Light

The Module 3 finale. Clean, even skin is a canvas — dodge & burn is where you paint light back onto it. By selectively brightening (dodge) and darkening (burn), you sculpt cheekbones, deepen eyes, and give a flat face dimension. It's the most artistic retouching skill, and the one that separates a snapshot from a portrait.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Sculpt a face by dodging (lightening) and burning (darkening)
  • Do it non-destructively on a 50% gray Soft Light layer
  • Add subtle life to eyes and teeth with masked adjustments
  • Finish and export a complete, natural portrait retouch

🌗 The Goal: Dimension from Light

Same face, same skin. The right side is evenly lit and a little flat; the left has been sculpted: brighter down the nose and cheekbones, gently shadowed at the edges. Drag the handle and watch the face gain depth:

🧠 Mental Model: Painting with Light

Photographers have "dodged" and "burned" since the darkroom: hold back light to lighten an area (dodge), add light to darken it (burn). In Photoshop it's the same idea: you're a lighting artist adding highlights and shadows after the shot.

Faces read as three-dimensional because of how light falls on them: the brow, nose bridge, cheekbones, and chin catch light; the sides, eye sockets, and under the jaw fall into shadow. Dodge the catch-light areas, burn the shadow areas, and a flat face pops into 3D.

🧠 The whole course, converging

Look how much is coming together: this is non-destructive (Module 0), it's black & white deciding an effect just like a mask (Module 1), and you can also do it with a masked Curves layer (Module 2). Dodge & burn isn't a new idea. It's your existing ideas aimed at light.

🛠️ Guided Build: Sculpt & Finish

Open your evened-out portrait from Lesson 3.2. Time to add light and finish.

Step 1: Make the gray Soft Light layer · 4 min

  1. Alt/⌥-click the New Layer button. In the dialog set Mode: Soft Light and tick Fill with Soft-Light-neutral color (50% gray). Name it D&B.
  2. It appears invisible — that's correct. Gray vanishes in Soft Light.

Step 2: Dodge the highlights · 8 min

  1. Brush (B), color white, Opacity ~10%, soft, large.
  2. Gently paint the natural catch-light zones: forehead center, down the nose bridge, tops of the cheekbones, the chin, and a little on the brow bones. Build up slowly with repeated light strokes.
💡 Low opacity is the secret. Ten percent, many passes. You can always add more; you can't un-see a harsh streak.

Step 3: Burn the shadows · 6 min

  1. Press X to switch to black, same low opacity.
  2. Deepen the natural shadow zones: the sides of the face, the temples, under the cheekbones, under the jaw, the eye sockets. This is what carves out cheekbones.

⚠️ Follow the existing light

Don't invent lighting from scratch — enhance what the photo already has. If the light came from the left, dodge the left-facing planes and burn the right. Fighting the original light looks wrong instantly.

Step 4: Eyes, teeth, and finish · 8 min

  1. Add a Curves adjustment layer, brighten slightly, fill its mask black, then paint white over just the whites of the eyes and the teeth. A touch of brightness there adds instant life. Keep it subtle. Teeth should look clean, not glowing.
  2. Optional: a second tiny Curves layer, masked to the iris, to add a little contrast/sparkle to the eyes.
  3. Final look at fit-to-screen. Save the .psd, then Export As ▸ JPG 80% → portrait-final.jpg. Module 3 deliverable: a natural portrait retouch, done. 🏆

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Dodge & burn a portrait using only the gray layer — no eye/teeth step — and see how far light alone carries it.
  2. Toggle the D&B layer's 👁 for a before/after. Then double the effect (duplicate the layer) to see "too much," and dial its opacity back to taste.
  3. Try burning a subtle vignette around the whole frame on the same layer to draw the eye to the face.

Going further: switch the gray layer's thumbnail view (Alt-click it) to see your white/black strokes on gray. Watching the "map" of your light is how you learn to sculpt faster.

🍳 Recipe Card: Dodge & Burn

Sculpt a face with light

  1. Alt/⌥-New Layer → Mode Soft Light, fill 50% gray
  2. Soft brush, ~10% opacity: paint white to dodge highlights
  3. X to black: burn the shadow planes
  4. Follow the photo's existing light; build up slowly
  5. Masked Curves on eyes/teeth → Save .psd → Export JPG

Mantra: dodge the light, burn the shadow, 10% at a time.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Dodge & Burn tools vs. the gray layer: which is better?

The gray Soft Light layer is non-destructive and re-editable, so it's the pro default. The Dodge/Burn tools paint directly on pixels (destructive), fine on a duplicate layer for a quick fix, but the gray layer wins for real work.

My sculpting looks smudgy or fake.

Usually opacity too high or brush too small/hard. Drop to ~10% opacity, use a big soft brush, and follow the face's real planes. Subtlety reads as skill.

How do I know where the highlights and shadows go?

Look at the light already in the photo and exaggerate it a little. Studying real portrait lighting (and even classical painting) trains your eye faster than any slider.
