Module 3 • Lesson 1🧑‍🎨 Natural Skin: Heal, Don't Erase

Welcome to the Practitioner tier. Module 3 is about people, and the golden rule of portrait work is that great retouching is invisible. Your subject should look like themselves on their best day, not like a wax figure. We start with the everyday skill: cleanly removing the temporary stuff.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Retouch skin non-destructively on a separate empty layer
  • Remove temporary blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush and Healing Brush
  • Decide what to fix and what to leave: the ethics of a natural retouch
  • Dial back your work with layer opacity so it never looks overdone

⏱️ Estimated Time: 50 minutes

🎯 Project: Retouch a portrait so it looks clean and natural, and pass the "would they recognize themselves?" test.

👤 The Goal: Invisible Retouching

🧠 Mental Model: Temporary vs. Permanent

Here's the single decision that keeps a retouch natural. Before you touch anything, sort every "flaw" into two buckets:

  • Temporary — things that wouldn't be there next week: a spot, a stray hair, a bit of shine, a piece of lint. Remove these freely.
  • Permanent — the things that make the person them: freckles, moles, laugh lines, scars, the shape of their nose. Leave these alone (or reduce, never erase).

Retouch the temporary; respect the permanent. Do that and your work will look like a good photo, not a filter.

🧠 Why this matters: beyond aesthetics

Portrait and beauty retouching is real, paid work, but it carries responsibility. Over-smoothing sets impossible standards and, frankly, looks dated and amateur. The industry has moved decisively toward natural skin. Learning restraint now makes your work both more ethical and more employable.

🛠️ Guided Build: Retouch a Portrait

Open a portrait (a friend, or a self-portrait). File ▸ Save As ▸ portrait-working.psd.

Step 1: Survey first, tools later · 5 min

  1. Zoom to 100% and slowly pan across the face. Don't touch anything yet.
  2. Mentally sort what you see into temporary (heal) and permanent (keep), using Figure 1.
💡 This 60-second survey is what separates a natural result from an over-edited one. Decide before you act.

Step 2: Make a retouch layer · 2 min

  1. New empty layer, name it retouch.
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J): set Type: Content-Aware and check Sample All Layers.

Step 3: Heal the temporary blemishes · 10 min

  1. Size the brush just larger than each blemish and single-click it. Work only on your "temporary" list.
  2. For a spot next to an edge (like the hairline), switch to the Healing Brush (same J group; Shift + J cycles through it). In the options bar set Sample: Current & Below (or All Layers): it's a separate setting from the Spot Healing Brush, and on the default Current Layer it would sample your empty layer. Alt/⌥-click clean skin nearby to sample, then click the blemish. Because you choose the source, it's much less likely to smear the edge; if it still does, finish that spot with the Clone Stamp (S, same Sample setting).

⚠️ Resist the "clean sweep"

It's tempting to remove every freckle and line "while you're in there." Don't. Those are the permanent bucket. Removing them is what makes a face look plastic.

Step 4: Dial it back & check · 5 min

  1. Lower the retouch layer's Opacity to ~85–90%. Letting a whisper of the original through keeps it believable.
  2. Toggle the layer's 👁 off/on to compare. If the retouched ("on") version still clearly looks like the same person on a good day, you nailed it.
  3. Save the .psd. (We'll build on this file across Module 3.)

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ All healing is on a separate retouch layer (tools sampling all layers)
  • ☐ Temporary blemishes are gone
  • ☐ Freckles, moles, and lines are still there
  • ☐ The retouch layer opacity is slightly under 100%
  • ☐ With the retouch layer on, it still looks like the same person

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Retouch a portrait twice on two copies: once tastefully (temporary only), once deliberately overdone (erase everything).
  2. Put them side by side. The overdone one will look worse — internalize why.
  3. On the tasteful version, find one distracting stray hair crossing the face and remove it with the Healing Brush.

Going further: a blemish sits on a permanent freckle you want to keep? Reduce it instead: heal it on its own new layer, then lower that layer's opacity so a hint returns. (Lowering the main retouch layer would fade every heal at once.) Reduction, not erasure.

🍳 Recipe Card: Clean Skin

Natural blemish removal

  1. Survey at 100% → sort temporary vs permanent
  2. New retouch layer → Spot Healing Sample All Layers ✓; Healing Brush Sample: Current & Below
  3. Spot Healing (J): click temporary blemishes
  4. Near edges → Healing Brush (Alt/⌥-sample clean skin)
  5. Layer opacity ~85–90% → toggle to sanity-check

Mantra: heal the temporary, keep the permanent.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Great retouching is invisible: the person looks like themselves on a good day.
  • Sort flaws into temporary (heal) and permanent (keep).
  • Work on a separate retouch layer, with each healing tool set to sample the layers below.
  • Use opacity to dial the effect back so it never looks overdone.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

You can now clean up a portrait the way working retouchers do: quickly, non-destructively, and with the restraint that keeps it looking real. That judgment (what to leave) is rarer and more valuable than the button-pushing.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Spot Healing vs. Healing Brush: when do I use which?

Spot Healing is one-click and picks its own source, perfect for isolated blemishes in open skin. The Healing Brush lets you choose the source (Alt-click). Use it near edges, hairlines, and anywhere Spot Healing smears.

The heal left a slightly different color patch.

Healing blends texture and color from nearby — if it sampled a shadow or a highlight, the color shifts. Sample from skin of the same brightness, or use the Healing Brush to pick the source deliberately.

How much retouching is "too much"?

If someone who knows the subject would say "that doesn't quite look like them," you've gone too far. The temporary-vs-permanent rule keeps you on the right side of that line.

🔭 Looking Ahead

Healing fixes spots, but it can't even out blotchy skin tone, and the obvious alternative, blurring the skin, turns it to plastic because blur smooths texture and color together. Lesson 3.2: Frequency Separation solves exactly that, by splitting texture from color so you can smooth one without harming the other. It's the technique that makes pro retouching possible.
