Module 1 • Lesson 2✂️ Selections That Hold Up

Last lesson you painted a mask by hand. That's perfect for soft, sweeping regions like a sky — but hopeless for a person's hair or the crisp edge of a bottle. Today you let Photoshop find the edge for you, then bake that selection into a clean mask you can trust.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Explain the difference between a selection (a temporary request) and a mask (the saved answer)
  • Select a subject automatically with Select Subject and the Object Selection tool
  • Rescue tricky edges (hair, fur, motion) in the Select & Mask workspace
  • Output a selection to a layer mask so your cutout stays non-destructive and re-editable

🎯 The Goal: A Believable Cutout

A busy background fights your subject for attention. The fix is to select the subject precisely, then drop it onto something calmer. Drag the handle — same subject, quieter backdrop:

🧠 Mental Model: A Selection Is a Request, a Mask Is the Saved Answer

When you make a selection, Photoshop shows "marching ants", a shimmering dotted outline. That outline is temporary. It's you pointing and saying "these pixels, please." The moment you click elsewhere or deselect, it's gone.

A mask is what happens when you write that request down permanently. Last lesson you learned masks are grayscale stencils: black hides, white reveals. A selection converts to a mask in one step: everything inside the marching ants becomes white (revealed), everything outside becomes black (hidden). Now your careful selection is saved, editable, and non-destructive. You can even paint on it later to fix a stray edge.

🧠 Why this reframe matters

Beginners obsess over getting the selection perfect, then delete the background, destroying pixels they can never get back. Pros make a good-enough selection, turn it into a mask, and refine the mask forever after. Same tools, totally different safety net. You're learning the pro way from the start.

🛠️ Guided Build: Cut Out a Subject

Open a photo with one clear subject: a person, a pet, a product. Save it as cutout-working.psd first (you know the drill).

Step 1: Let AI take the first swing · 4 min

  1. With your photo layer active, go to Select ▸ Subject (or click Select Subject in the options bar of the Object Selection, Quick Selection, or Magic Wand tool).
  2. Marching ants appear around your subject. It won't be perfect, and that's expected and fine.

Step 2: Patch the obvious misses · 6 min

  1. Grab the Quick Selection tool (W; it shares a slot with Object Selection and Magic Wand, so press Shift + W to cycle if a different one appears).
  2. Paint over any bit of subject the AI missed to add it.
  3. Hold Alt/⌥ and paint over background that got included to subtract it.
💡 Don't chase perfection here. Get the big shape right; we fix the fiddly edges in the next step, which is built exactly for that.

Step 3: Refine the edge in Select & Mask · 10 min

  1. Click Select and Mask… in the options bar. The workspace from Figure 2 opens.
  2. Set View to Overlay so masked-out areas show as a red film.
  3. For hair or fur: pick the Refine Edge Brush and gently paint along the wispy edge, and Photoshop rebuilds those strands.
  4. Nudge Smooth and Feather a touch for a natural edge; use Shift Edge slightly negative to bite off any leftover background halo.
  5. Still seeing a colored fringe from the old background? In Output Settings, check Decontaminate Colors. (It switches the output to New Layer with Layer Mask, which is still fully non-destructive: your original layer is kept, just hidden.)

⚠️ The tell-tale sign of a bad cutout

A bright halo or a razor-sharp edge screams "cut out." A believable edge is slightly soft and, on hair, slightly translucent. Feather, Shift Edge, and Decontaminate Colors are how you kill the halo.

Step 4: Output to a Layer Mask (not a delete!) · 4 min

  1. At the bottom right, set Output To: Layer Mask.
  2. Click OK. Your photo layer now has a mask hiding the background, exactly the black/white card from Lesson 1.1.
  3. Add a new layer below it: Ctrl/⌘-click the New Layer icon in the Layers panel (new layers normally appear above), or drag the new layer down. Fill it with a calm color (Edit ▸ Fill…) or place another photo there. Your subject now sits on the new background.
  4. File ▸ Save (Ctrl/⌘ + S) to keep your layered cutout-working.psd.

✅ The payoff

Click the mask and paint (black hides / white reveals) to fix any stray pixel — forever. You never deleted a thing. If tomorrow you want the old background back, Shift-click the mask thumbnail to turn the mask off. That's the whole point of masks over erasing.

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ My subject is selected and the background is hidden by a layer mask (not erased)
  • ☐ The edge has no bright halo and isn't razor-sharp
  • ☐ Hair/fur edges look soft, not chopped
  • ☐ There's a new, calmer background layer beneath the subject
  • ☐ I can still paint the mask to fix strays
  • ☐ I saved my layered .psd

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Cut out a harder subject: something with wispy hair, fur, or a leafy plant.
  2. Lean on the Refine Edge Brush for the wispy parts; don't try to paint them by hand.
  3. Place your cutout on a photo background of a different scene. Do the colors clash? Note it: matching light between subject and background is exactly what Module 4 (Compositing) is about.

Going further: select your subject, then choose Select ▸ Inverse (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I). Now the background is selected instead. Feel how a selection is just a boundary you can flip.

🍳 Recipe Card: Select → Mask

Believable cutout, every time

  1. Select ▸ Subject (let AI start)
  2. Quick Selection (W): paint to add, Alt/⌥-paint to subtract
  3. Select and Mask… → Refine Edge Brush on hair; nudge Smooth / Feather / Shift Edge (Decontaminate Colors for fringes)
  4. Output To: Layer Mask → OK
  5. New background layer beneath; paint the mask to fix strays; File ▸ Save

Mantra: mask it, don't erase it.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • A selection (marching ants) is temporary; a mask is the saved, editable answer.
  • Select Subject and Object Selection get you 90% there in one click.
  • Select & Mask is where believable edges — especially hair — are won.
  • Always Output To: Layer Mask. Mask it, don't erase it.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

Cutting a subject out cleanly is one of the most-requested skills in all of Photoshop. It's behind every product shot, poster, and composite you've ever admired. You can now do it the safe, professional way, halo-free, with an edge that survives a second look.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

Select Subject grabbed the wrong thing. Is the tool broken?

No. It guesses the most prominent subject. On a busy photo, use the Object Selection tool and draw a box around exactly the thing you want; it'll snap to that object instead.

My hair edges still look chunky.

Use the Refine Edge Brush inside Select & Mask and paint along the hairline, not the solid interior. That brush is specifically built to reconstruct fine strands; a normal brush can't.

Should I ever just use the Eraser?

Almost never for cutouts. The Eraser destroys pixels permanently. A mask does the same thing visually but stays reversible, which is why every technique in this course reaches for a mask instead.
