Module 6 • Lesson 2↔️ Generative Expand & Reframe

Sometimes the problem is not inside the photo, it is the edges of it: a subject cropped too tight, a portrait you need as a landscape, headroom for a title. Generative Expand extends the canvas past the original photo and invents believable new space to fill it.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Extend the canvas with the Crop tool and fill the new space generatively
  • Uncrop a too-tight photo to give a subject room to breathe
  • Reframe a photo to a new aspect ratio for print, web, or social
  • Guide the fill with an optional prompt and keep the seam invisible

⏱️ Estimated Time: 45 minutes

🎯 Project: Take a tightly-cropped photo and expand it into a roomier, better-composed frame.

🏔️ The Goal: Room to Breathe

Same mountain photo. On the right it is cramped into a narrow frame, with gray canvas where there is no image. On the left, the scene has been expanded to fill that space, seamlessly. The dashed lines mark where the original photo ended. Drag the handle:

🧠 Mental Model: Paint Past the Edge

Generative Fill invents content inside your photo. Generative Expand does the same trick outside it: you make the canvas bigger than the image, which leaves empty space, and Photoshop generates scenery to fill that space so it continues the photo seamlessly.

That single ability solves a surprising number of real problems:

  • Too-tight crop. A subject jammed against the edge gets breathing room.
  • Wrong aspect ratio. A vertical phone photo becomes a wide banner without squishing anything.
  • Space for design. Add clean sky or floor for a headline to sit on, connecting straight to your Module 5 poster work.

🧠 It is still just Generative Fill

Under the hood, Expand is Generative Fill aimed at the empty canvas around your image. Same generative layer, same variations, same optional prompt. If you understood 6.1, you already understand 6.2. This is the spiral tightening: one idea, a new frame.

🛠️ Guided Build: Uncrop & Reframe

Open a photo that feels cramped or is the wrong shape for where you need it. Save as expand-working.psd.

Step 1: Uncrop for breathing room · 8 min

  1. Grab the Crop tool (C) and set Fill: Generative Expand in the options bar.
  2. Drag a crop handle outward past the edge to add empty canvas where the subject needs room.
  3. Press Enter, leave the prompt empty, and Generate. Photoshop continues the scene into the new space.

Step 2: Reframe to a new ratio · 8 min

  1. With the Crop tool, pick a target Ratio (16:9 for a banner, 1:1 for social, 4:5 for a feed post).
  2. The crop box now includes empty areas to reach that shape. Generate to fill them and your vertical photo becomes a clean horizontal, no stretching.

⚠️ Expand in bites

Huge single expansions get vague and repetitive. For a big change, expand partway, generate, then expand again. Several smaller expansions look far better than one enormous leap.

Step 3: Guide it with a prompt (optional) · 5 min

  1. Leave the prompt empty to simply continue the scene. Or type a short prompt to steer the new area: a calm beach, more forest, open sky.
  2. Cycle the variations and pick the one whose horizon, light, and texture line up with the original.

Step 4: Hide the seam · 5 min

  1. Zoom to 100% along the join between original and generated. If anything repeats or misaligns, select that strip and regenerate just it (6.1).
  2. A tiny clipped Curves or a whisper of the Module 4 unifying grade over the whole frame blends old and new into one image.

✅ The horizon test

The fastest giveaway of a bad expand is a horizon or a strong line that jumps at the seam. Check that first. If the horizon runs straight through, most viewers will never suspect the edges were invented.

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ The canvas was expanded past the original with Generative Expand
  • ☐ The subject now has appropriate breathing room
  • ☐ The photo reaches a useful new aspect ratio with no stretching
  • ☐ Horizons and strong lines run continuously across the seam
  • ☐ A subtle grade unifies original and generated areas

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Take one vertical phone photo and produce three versions from it: a 1:1 square, a 16:9 banner, and a tall 9:16 story, all by expanding rather than cropping away.
  2. Expand a portrait to add clean headroom, then drop a title into that new space using your Module 5 type skills.
  3. Deliberately over-expand in one huge step and see it go vague, then redo it in three smaller steps. Feel the difference.

Going further: combine 6.1 and 6.2 on one image: expand the frame, then generatively add a new element into the space you just created.

🍳 Recipe Card: Generative Expand

Uncrop and reframe

  1. Crop tool (C), set Fill: Generative Expand
  2. Drag handles outward, or pick a target Ratio
  3. Enter → empty prompt (continue) or a short prompt (steer) → Generate
  4. Expand in bites for big changes; pick the best variation
  5. Check the seam at 100%; unify with a subtle grade

Mantra: crop outward, generate the margin, keep the horizon honest.

📝 Lesson Summary

🎓 Key Takeaways

  • Generative Expand is Generative Fill aimed at empty canvas around the photo.
  • Use the Crop tool set to Fill: Generative Expand, dragging outward or picking a ratio.
  • Expand in bites, and keep horizons continuous across the seam.
  • Great for uncropping, reframing aspect ratios, and adding space for design.

🎉 What You've Accomplished

You are no longer limited to the pixels the camera captured. You can give a subject room, turn one photo into every aspect ratio a project needs, and manufacture clean space for type. That is a genuine production superpower, especially for social and design work.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

My expanded area looks repetitive or smeary.

You expanded too far in one go. Undo, expand a smaller amount, generate, then repeat. Also try adding a short prompt to give the fill something specific to build.

The horizon does not line up at the edge.

Cycle the variations; usually one lines up better. If none do, select a thin strip across the seam and regenerate just that with Generative Fill to stitch it.

Can I expand a photo forever?

Technically yes, but quality and believability drop the farther you get from real pixels. Expand for composition and framing, not to invent whole landscapes from a postage stamp.
