🎬 The Goal: One Photograph

🧠 Mental Model: Shadow, Depth, Unity

Three things convince the eye that separate images are one photograph, and they work at three different scales:

  • Shadow (the object). Nothing real floats. A contact shadow where the subject meets the ground is the strongest single cue that it belongs. A softer cast shadow in the direction the light throws it adds even more.
  • Depth (the space). Air is not perfectly clear. Distant things are hazier, lighter, and lower in contrast than near things. A little atmospheric perspective restores that sense of space.
  • Unity (the whole). A single grade laid over everything gives the final image one shared color and mood, the way a single camera and lens would.

🧠 The whole course, in one image

Look at what this finale draws on: non-destructive layers (Module 0), masks (Module 1), grading with Curves (Module 2), and shadow-painting that is really dodge and burn (Module 3). A finished composite is every skill you have built, working together.

The order of layers matters here. The shadow goes under the subject; the unifying grade goes over everything. Here is the finished stack, drawn as a diagram:

🛠️ Guided Build: Ground, Depth, Grade

Open your matched composite from Lesson 4.2.

Step 1: Paint a contact shadow · 10 min

  1. Make a new layer below the subject. With a soft black brush at low opacity, paint a small, darker pool right where the subject touches the ground.
  2. Keep the contact point (feet, base) darkest and tightest. That first centimetre of shadow is what sells contact.

Step 2: Add a cast shadow · 8 min

  1. On the same layer, paint a longer, softer shadow stretching away from the light. If the sun is high-right, the shadow falls low-left.
  2. A cast shadow gets softer and lighter the farther it travels from the subject. Lower the brush opacity as you move outward, and blur the layer with a little Gaussian Blur.

⚠️ One light, one shadow direction

Every shadow in the scene must fall the same way, because there is one sun. A subject shadow pointing the wrong direction is as jarring as a bad edge. Check the shadows already in the background and match them.

Step 3: Add atmosphere for depth · 6 min

  1. If the subject sits in the mid or far distance, add a whisper of the sky's color between it and the camera. A low-opacity soft layer of the scene's haze color, masked to the distance, pushes it back into space.
  2. Near subjects get little or none. The amount of haze is the sense of distance.

Step 4: Unify and export · 8 min

  1. At the very top of the stack, add one grade over everything: a Color Lookup or a gentle Curves that gives the whole image one shared mood. A subtle warm wash and a soft vignette work wonders.
  2. Because this layer sits over both subject and background, it forces them to share a final look, the way a single camera would.
  3. Final squint test. File ▸ Save the layered master, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG. Module 4 deliverable: one believable photograph. 🏆

  • ☐ A dark, tight contact shadow sits where the subject meets the ground

🧗 Now You: Solo Variation

🌟 Your challenge

  1. Finish a composite with a hard light source (bright sun) and again with a soft one (overcast). Notice how the shadow changes: hard light gives a sharp, dark shadow; soft light gives a faint, fuzzy one.
  2. Deliberately point your subject's shadow the wrong way, look at how instantly wrong it feels, then fix it. Feeling the error cements the rule.
  3. Grade the same finished composite two ways: warm and golden, then cool and moody. One image, two moods.

Going further: reflected light. Real surfaces bounce color up onto a subject (green grass tints the underside of a chin). A tiny bit of bounced scene-color on your subject's shadow side is the master's touch.

🍳 Recipe Card: Finish a Composite

Ground, depth, unity

  1. New layer below subject: paint a tight, dark contact shadow at the feet
  2. Add a softer cast shadow in the light's direction; blur it
  3. Add subtle atmosphere (haze) for distance
  4. Top of stack: one unifying grade + soft vignette over everything
  5. Squint test, save master, export JPG

Mantra: shadow under, grade over, one light for all.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

My shadow looks fake. What is wrong?

Usually it is too uniform. Real shadows are darkest and sharpest at the contact point and get softer and lighter as they travel. Vary the opacity and softness along the shadow's length.

How much atmosphere is right?

Less than you think. Atmosphere is a whisper. If you can clearly see the haze layer, it is too strong. Back it off until the depth reads without the effect announcing itself.

Should the unifying grade be strong?

No. Its job is cohesion, not drama. A subtle shared color and a gentle vignette are usually enough. If the grade is doing the heavy lifting, revisit your light and color match first.
