L01

Fix & Export One Photo

guided + independent pass
By the end you can
  • Open a photo and straighten & crop it with the Crop tool
  • Improve its brightness and contrast with a Brightness/Contrast adjustment layer
  • Explain, in one sentence, what non-destructive editing means and why it matters
  • Export a finished JPEG without ever altering your original file
Key ideas
  • Tracing paper. Instead of drawing on the photo with a marker, you lay clear sheets over it and make your changes there. Hate a change? Peel that sheet off. The photo underneath is never touched.
  • In Photoshop those sheets are layers. Special ones that only carry an instruction ("make everything under me brighter") are adjustment layers.
  • Non-destructive editing = working so the original is always intact. It's the actual professional workflow, not a beginner shortcut.
  • Four places on screen today: Tools (left edge), Options bar (along the top), Canvas (the big middle), Layers panel (bottom-right). Missing the Layers panel? Window ▸ Layers or F7.
  • Save vs. Export. Save writes your layered working file (.psd) so you can keep editing. Export flattens everything into one share-ready image (.jpg). Never post the .psd; never edit on the .jpg.
Worked example: the guided build
  1. Open & save a working file. File ▸ Open your photo. Immediately File ▸ Save As, format Photoshop (.psd), name it myphoto-working.psd. (If asked where, choose Save on your computer.)
  2. Straighten & crop. Press C for the Crop tool. Drag a corner inward to remove distracting edges. To straighten, move the cursor just outside a corner until it becomes a bent arrow, then drag to rotate until the grid lines match the horizon. Press Enter (Return).
  3. Add ONE adjustment layer. At the bottom of the Layers panel click ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast. Nudge Brightness up until the shadows open; nudge Contrast up a little. Toggle the eyeball next to the new layer off and on: the edit vanishes and returns.
  4. Save, then export. File ▸ Save (Ctrl/⌘ + S). Then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As…, format JPG, quality about 80%, and save as myphoto-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Quick Fix & Export (the 5-step loop)
  1. Open → Save As .psd (protect the original)
  2. C Crop & straighten → Enter
  3. Layers panel ▸ ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast → nudge
  4. (Optional) ◐ ▸ Vibrance → nudge
  5. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80%

Golden rule: edits live on adjustment layers, never on the photo.

L02

The Layer Stack & Your First Mask

50 min
By the end you can
  • Read a layer stack: what's on top wins, and why order matters
  • Explain a mask in one sentence: black hides, white reveals, gray partly reveals
  • Paint on a layer mask to apply an adjustment to only part of a photo
  • Recover from mistakes by painting the mask back: no undo panic required
Key ideas
  • The stack of glass. Layers are sheets of glass in a frame; you look straight down. Higher layers win where they're opaque. Order is meaning: move a sheet and the picture changes.
  • Every adjustment layer comes with a layer mask: a grayscale card that decides, pixel by pixel, how much of the effect gets through.
  • Black hides. White reveals. Gray does a bit of both.
  • An adjustment layer shows two thumbnails: the effect (◐) and the mask (the white/black card). Click the mask thumbnail before you paint.
  • Masks make mistakes painless: press X to swap brush color and paint the mistake back. That's why pros mask instead of erase.
Worked example: rescue the sky
  1. Open a landscape (or l02-washed-sky.jpg). File ▸ Save As ▸ sky-rescue-working.psd.
  2. Layers panel ▸ ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast. Pull Brightness down until the sky looks rich. Ignore the land going dark.
  3. Click the mask thumbnail. Press D, then X so black is the foreground. Edit ▸ Fill… ▸ Contents: Black, or Alt/⌥ + Backspace/Delete. The darkening vanishes.
  4. Press B for the Brush, X for white, pick a big soft brush, and paint over the sky. The darkening comes back only where you paint.
  5. At the horizon, lower brush opacity to about 30% and blend gently. Toggle the eyeball for the before/after. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → sky-rescue-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Mask an adjustment to "only here"
  1. Add adjustment layer (◐) → push it too far on purpose
  2. Click the mask thumbnail
  3. Fill black (hide it everywhere): Alt/⌥ + Backspace with black foreground
  4. Brush white (B, soft, big) over the area you want the effect
  5. Mistakes → press X to swap to black, paint them out

Mantra: black hides, white reveals, gray blends.

L03

Selections That Hold Up

50 min
By the end you can
  • Explain the difference between a selection (a temporary request) and a mask (the saved answer)
  • Select a subject automatically with Select Subject and the Object Selection tool
  • Rescue tricky edges (hair, fur, motion) in the Select & Mask workspace
  • Output a selection to a layer mask so your cutout stays non-destructive and re-editable
Key ideas
  • A selection shows marching ants. It's temporary: you pointing and saying "these pixels, please." Click elsewhere and it's gone.
  • A mask is the request written down permanently: inside the ants becomes white (revealed), outside becomes black (hidden).
  • Pros make a good-enough selection, turn it into a mask, and refine the mask forever after. Mask it, don't erase it.

Worked example: cut out a subject
  1. Open a photo with one clear subject (or l03-subject-busy-bg.jpg). Save As cutout-working.psd.
  2. Select ▸ Subject. Marching ants appear. Not perfect? Expected and fine.
  3. Quick Selection (W; Shift + W cycles the slot): paint to add missed subject; hold Alt/⌥ and paint to subtract background.
  4. Select and Mask… in the options bar. View: Overlay. Refine Edge Brush along wispy edges. Nudge Smooth and Feather; Shift Edge slightly negative. Colored fringe? Check Decontaminate Colors.
  5. Output To: Layer Mask → OK. Add a layer beneath (Ctrl/⌘-click the New Layer icon), fill it with a calm color or place another photo there. File ▸ Save.
Recipe card: Select → Mask
  1. Select ▸ Subject (let AI start)
  2. Quick Selection (W): paint to add, Alt/⌥-paint to subtract
  3. Select and Mask… → Refine Edge Brush on hair; nudge Smooth / Feather / Shift Edge (Decontaminate Colors for fringes)
  4. Output To: Layer Mask → OK
  5. New background layer beneath; paint the mask to fix strays; File ▸ Save

Mantra: mask it, don't erase it.

L04

Cleaning Up: Spots, Distractions & Edges

50 min
By the end you can
  • Remove small spots and blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush
  • Choose between healing (blends texture) and cloning (copies exactly), and know when each wins
  • Erase a larger distraction with Content-Aware Fill or the Remove tool
  • Work non-destructively by healing on a separate layer, then export your finished image
Key ideas
  • Every cleanup tool borrows good pixels to cover bad ones. They differ only in how they blend.
  • Cloning copies exactly: great for hard edges and patterns, ruthless if the lighting doesn't match.
  • Healing copies texture, then blends color and brightness into the surroundings: magic on skin and skies.
  • Content-Aware tools do it automatically over a whole region.
  • The habit: clean up on an empty layer, with every tool sampling all layers: the Sample All Layers checkbox (Spot Healing Brush, Remove tool) or the Sample: menu set to Current & Below or All Layers (Healing Brush, Clone Stamp).
Worked example: clean & export
  1. Open your messy photo (or l04-dusty-sky.jpg). Save As cleanup-working.psd. Click the New Layer icon; name it retouch.
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J), check Sample All Layers. Zoom to 100% (Ctrl/⌘ + 1). Brush just a little larger than each spot; single-click it. Work left to right, top to bottom.
  3. Big distraction, Option A: Remove tool (healing group), Sample All Layers checked, brush over the whole object, release. Option B: click the photo layer, Lasso (L) a loose selection with margin, Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill…, green-brush the areas to sample from, Output To: New Layer, OK. Then click retouch again.
  4. Stubborn hard edge: Clone Stamp (S), Sample: Current & Below. Alt/⌥-click a clean area, then paint over the flaw.
  5. Final 100% pass. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → cleanup-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Clean a photo (distraction-free in five moves)
  1. New empty layer named retouch → every tool samples all layers (checkbox or Sample: menu)
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J): click each tiny spot at 100% zoom
  3. Big object → Remove tool (brush over) or Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill
  4. Stubborn edge → Clone Stamp (S, Sample: Current & Below, Alt/⌥-sample)
  5. 100% final pass → Save .psd → Export JPG

Mantra: heal to blend, clone to copy, always on a fresh layer.

L05

The Develop Step: Camera Raw

42 min
By the end you can
  • Open a photo as a Smart Object so Camera Raw becomes a re-editable Smart Filter
  • Fix a color cast with White Balance (Temperature and Tint)
  • Set global tone with Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks
  • Explain why you develop globally first, before any local edits
Key ideas
  • Camera Raw is the develop step: the digital darkroom where you get the raw light honest before any creative choice.
  • Global before local. Fix the whole image (white balance, overall tone) first. Dodging and burning a photo that still has an orange cast is just polishing a problem.
  • Convert the layer to a Smart Object first. Then Camera Raw attaches as a Smart Filter you can double-click and re-edit forever.
  • Work the panel top to bottom: White Balance, then Exposure → Contrast → Highlights/Shadows → Whites/Blacks.
  • Clipping means a highlight has gone pure white (or a shadow pure black) with no detail left. Hold Alt / ⌥ while dragging Whites or Blacks to see exactly where it happens.

Curves: Precise Tone & Color

50 min
By the end you can
  • Read the Curves graph: input across, output up, the diagonal is "no change"
  • Add contrast with an S-curve, and flatten it for a soft, filmic look
  • Color-grade by bending the Red, Green and Blue channels
  • Apply a Curves adjustment layer to only part of the photo with a mask
Key ideas
  • A curve is a tone translator. Tones walk in along the bottom (input) and out up the side (output). The straight diagonal means "leave it alone."
  • Bend a point up and those tones get brighter; bend it down and they get darker. You choose which tones: shadows, midtones, or highlights.
  • S-curve = shadows down, highlights up = more contrast. Lifted bottom-left corner = milky, faded blacks (matte, vintage).
  • Channel curves grade color. In the Blue channel, shadows up + highlights down = cool shadows, warm highlights. Add Red shadows down for a truer teal. Move in tiny amounts.
  • A masked Curves layer grades one region only: fill the mask black, paint white where you want the effect.
  • Curves layers go above your Camera Raw develop. Global first, again.

Smart Objects & Finishing the Grade

45 min
By the end you can
  • Explain what a Smart Object is: a protective container around your pixels
  • Scale, warp, and transform without losing quality
  • Apply Smart Filters that stay re-editable and maskable
  • Finish a fully non-destructive grade and export it
Key ideas
  • A Smart Object stores your pixels; everything you do to it is a re-editable instruction, not a permanent change.
  • Scale a normal layer down and back up and it turns to mush: the pixels were thrown away. A Smart Object re-renders crisp from the sealed original.
  • Any filter on a Smart Object nests beneath it as a Smart Filter. Filters share one filter mask, the newest sits on top, and they apply from the bottom up.
  • Paint the filter mask black to hide the filter in an area (black hides, white reveals).
  • Don't heal or clone directly on a Smart Object: those tools want normal pixels. Retouch first, then convert.
  • Keep the layered .psd as your master; export a flat JPEG as the deliverable.
Worked example: finish the grade
  1. Open grade-working.psd. Check the photo layer's thumbnail for the page-fold badge (if missing: right-click ▸ Convert to Smart Object).

L09

Frequency Separation

55 min
By the end you can
  • Explain high frequency (texture) vs low frequency (color and tone)
  • Build the setup with Gaussian Blur + Apply Image
  • Even out blotchy skin tone on the color layer, texture untouched
  • Fix stray texture on the texture layer, color untouched
Key ideas
  • Every image is two things layered together. Low frequency: the slow, soft stuff (overall color, shading, blotches): the paint. High frequency: the fast, fine stuff (pores, fine hairs, crisp edges): the texture stamped into it.
  • Blurring a face smooths both at once, which is why it looks plastic. Frequency separation puts them on two layers so you can fix one without harming the other.
  • LOW = a blurred copy (Normal mode). HIGH = the texture extracted with Apply Image, in Linear Light mode, on top. Together they look identical to the original.
  • Color on LOW, texture on HIGH: never mix them.
  • The Apply Image recipe depends on bit depth (Image ▸ Mode). 8-bit: Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128. 16-bit: Add, Scale 2, Offset 0, Invert.
Worked example: the recipe card
  1. Open your portrait from L08. Stamp Visible: Ctrl + Alt + Shift + E / ⌘ + ⌥ + ⇧ + E.
  2. Duplicate it twice (Ctrl/⌘ + J ×2). Lower copy = LOW, upper copy = HIGH. Hide HIGH.
  3. Select LOW: Filter ▸ Blur ▸ Gaussian Blur until blotches are gone and pores just disappear (often ~6–12 px).
  4. Show and select HIGH: Image ▸ Apply Image: Layer LOW, Blending Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128 (8-bit). OK. HIGH turns flat gray.
  5. Set HIGH's blend mode to Linear Light. The image looks normal again. ✅ Checkpoint.
  6. On LOW: Lasso a blotchy area, Select ▸ Modify ▸ Feather 10–20 px, small Gaussian Blur. Or paint with a soft brush at low opacity, sampling good nearby color.
  7. On HIGH: Healing Brush or Clone Stamp with Sample: Current Layer to remove a flyaway hair. Save.

  • Rescue fine hair with the Refine Edge Brush
  • Kill the fringe with Decontaminate Colors
  • Output a new layer with a layer mask, ready for any scene
Key ideas
  • The background tells on you. Edge pixels picked up color from whatever was behind the subject. On a new scene, that leftover color shows as a fringe (a halo).
  • A composite cutout has two jobs: choose the right pixels, and clean the old color out of the edge.
  • Decontaminate Colors strips that fringe. It rewrites edge pixels, so Photoshop forces the result onto a new layer; keep your original as a hidden backup.

  • Anchor a subject with a contact shadow and a soft cast shadow

Key ideas
  • Shadow (the object): nothing real floats. A contact shadow is the strongest single cue that a subject belongs.
  • Depth (the space): distant things are hazier, lighter, and lower in contrast. A whisper of haze restores distance.
  • Unity (the whole): one grade over everything gives the image a single shared look, like one camera and lens.
  • One light, one shadow direction. If the sun is high-right, shadows fall low-left.
Worked example: the finishing moves
  1. New layer below the subject, named Contact Shadow. Soft black brush, low opacity: a small, dark pool right where the feet touch the ground. Never blur this layer.
  2. New layer just below that, named Cast Shadow. A longer, softer shadow away from the light, lighter as it travels. Blur only this layer: Filter ▸ Blur ▸ Gaussian Blur.
  3. For a mid or far subject: a low-opacity soft layer of the scene's haze color, masked to the distance.
  4. At the very top: a Color Lookup or gentle Curves for one shared mood, plus a vignette (new layer, big soft black brush on the corners, lower the opacity).
  5. File ▸ Save the layered master. File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG (flattened automatically).

Key ideas
  • Raster (a photo) is a grid of pixels: enlarge it and it goes soft or blocky. Vector is stored as math, redrawn crisp at any size. Type is vector too.

  • Add new content by selecting an area and describing what belongs there

Key ideas
  • Select, then describe. The selection is the where; the prompt is the what.
  • Empty prompt = remove. Photoshop fills the selection with plausible background (a smarter cousin of Content-Aware Fill from Module 1).

  • Every result lands on a new generative layer with its own mask, with three variations to choose from. Your original pixels are never touched.
  • The selection's size and shape steer the result: want something tall, make a tall selection.

  4. Refine: it is a normal layer with a mask, so mask a rough edge (Module 1), match light and color with clipped adjustments (Module 4), heal a small artifact (Module 3).

Key ideas
  • Expand is Generative Fill aimed at empty canvas around your photo. Same generative layer, same variations, same optional prompt.

Worked example: uncrop & reframe
  1. Open a cramped photo. Save as expand-working.psd.
  2. Uncrop: Crop tool (C), set Fill: Generative Expand. Drag a handle outward past the edge. Leave the task bar prompt empty and click Generate. (Pressing Enter or the checkmark may also generate straight away with no prompt, so use the task bar when you want to type one.)
  3. Reframe: pick a Ratio (16:9 banner, 1:1 social, 4:5 feed post). Drag the handles outward until the box extends past the photo; the ratio stays locked. Generate.
  4. Guide it (optional): a short prompt steers the new area: a calm beach, more forest, open sky.
  5. Hide the seam: zoom to 100% along the join. Anything repeating or misaligned? Select that strip and regenerate just it (L17). Then a tiny clipped Curves or a whisper of the Module 4 unifying grade over the whole frame.

Actions, Batch & Droplets

45 min

  • Record, play, and edit an Action
  • Run an Action across a folder with Batch

  • Turn an Action into a drag-and-drop droplet
Key ideas
  • An Action is a recording of your steps. Record once, replay forever: on one file, a folder, or as a droplet.
  • Actions live in Window ▸ Actions, inside a Set. Red dot = record, square = stop, triangle = play.
  • Record general steps. Fit Image fits the long edge for any orientation; Image Size would record a fixed width.
  • Automate the boring, keep the creative. Resizing, converting, watermarking, and exporting are for Actions; grading and retouching are for you.
  • Rule of thumb: if you do it more than twice, record it.
Worked example: record "Web JPG 1200"

Put your test images in one folder and make an empty "processed" folder beside it.

  1. Open one image. In the Actions panel, make a new Set (Export Web), then a new Action (Web JPG 1200), and click Record in the New Action dialog.
  2. Edit ▸ Convert to Profile, destination sRGB IEC61966-2.1.
  3. File ▸ Automate ▸ Fit Image, 1200 × 1200 px.
  4. File ▸ Place Embedded your watermark and position it.
  5. Layer ▸ Flatten Image (Save As only offers JPEG for a file without extra layers).
  6. File ▸ Save As JPEG (quality 8 to 10 on the 0 to 12 scale) into the processed folder, then close the file.
  7. Press Stop. Open a different image, select the Action, press Play to test.
  8. File ▸ Automate ▸ Batch: choose the Set and Action, Source = input folder, Destination = processed folder, tick Override Action "Save As" Commands. Click OK.
