# Module 4 Labs — Compositing (Session 5)

Module 4 builds **one file across three lessons**: `composite-working.psd`. Learners cut a
subject out (L11), match it to a scene's light and color (L12), then ground it with
shadows, add depth, unify it with one grade, and export it (L13). The exported JPG is the
Module 4 Practitioner deliverable.

```
labs/module-4/
├─ README.md                           this file
└─ practice/                           synthetic practice files (generated by script, see specs below)
   ├─ l11-figure-on-green.png          main cutout subject (green fringe to decontaminate)
   ├─ l11-pet-on-blue-wall.png         fur-edge challenge subject (blue fringe)
   ├─ l11-figure-cutout-reference.png  instructor safety net: a clean, finished cutout
   ├─ l12-scene-sunset.jpg             warm, hazy scene, sun low on the right
   ├─ l12-scene-overcast.jpg           cool, soft, shadowless scene
   ├─ l12-subject-indoor-warm.png      "hard case": tungsten-lit subject for a daylight scene
   └─ l13-scene-hard-sun.jpg           bright midday scene, hard shadows, visible haze in the distance
```

## Bring your own photos (recommended)

Ask learners, in the pre-session email, to bring:

1. **A subject photo**: a person, pet, or product shot against a *plain-ish* background.
   Full-length (feet or base visible) works best, because L12 and L13 need the subject
   standing on the ground. Some hair or fur makes L11 more rewarding. A subject against a
   strongly colored background (green foliage, a blue wall) makes decontamination easy to see.
2. **A background scene** with a **clear light direction**: visible shadows on the ground
   tell them where the sun is. A sunset, a street, a field, a beach.
3. *(Optional)* A second scene with very different light (overcast vs. sunny) for the solo
   variation.

Learners may also download free-license photos from sites such as **Pexels** or
**Unsplash**, after checking the current license terms on the photo's page. Never pull
images from a web search.

Struggling learners should use the synthetic files: they are built to cut out cleanly.

## Practice file specs (for the generator script)

All files are sRGB. PNG files are RGB (no alpha) unless noted. JPG quality 90.
Coordinates are in pixels from the top-left. "Lit from the right" means the right side
of every form is lighter.

### L11 · Cut It Out Cleanly

**`l11-figure-on-green.png`** · 1600 × 2400 px
- **Background:** saturated grass green (`#3f9a3a`), with 40–60 soft, slightly darker
  and lighter green blobs (radius 60–200 px, Gaussian blurred ~25 px) so it reads as
  out-of-focus foliage, plus light noise (±6 per channel).
- **Subject:** a simple full-length standing figure, centered at x = 800, feet at
  y ≈ 2250, head top at y ≈ 330 (about 1920 px tall):
  - head: skin-tone ellipse (`#e0b08c`) 230 × 290 px;
  - hair: a dark-brown (`#4a2e1c`) cap over the top and sides of the head, plus
    **200+ wispy strands**: 1–2 px anti-aliased lines, 30–120 px long, radiating outward
    from the hair outline with slight random curvature, 40–80 % opacity;
  - torso: rounded rectangle 520 × 720 px in a saturated cobalt-blue shirt (`#2f6fe0`);
    arms as rounded rectangles hanging at the sides;
  - legs: dark charcoal trousers (`#2c2f38`), two columns with a small gap between them;
  - shoes: near-black rounded shapes 180 × 70 px, bottoms at y ≈ 2250.
- **Lighting:** cool, bright daylight from the **upper right**: apply a horizontal
  gradient over the figure only, +18 % brightness on the right edge to −12 % on the left.
  The figure should be clearly brighter and more saturated than the Module 4 scenes.
- **The flaw to fix (fringe):** along the entire silhouette, blend a 4 px band 50 % toward
  the background green; hair strands are tinted 35 % toward green. Result: a visible green
  halo at 200 % zoom when the subject is placed on another color.
- Soft, even edge anti-aliasing on the body (1–1.5 px); no hard aliasing.

**`l11-pet-on-blue-wall.png`** · 1600 × 1600 px
- **Background:** saturated blue wall (`#2f5fbf`) with faint vertical plank lines every
  160 px (1 px, 8 % darker) and light noise.
- **Subject:** a stylized sitting cat or dog in warm tan (`#c89a64`) with a lighter chest
  (`#e8cfa8`): body ellipse 700 × 820 px centered at (800, 1020), head circle radius
  220 px at (800, 560), two triangular ears, a curved tail wrapping to the front, base on
  a thin floor strip (`#8a7a66`, bottom 180 px of the image).
- **Fur edge:** the entire outline (except where it meets the floor) is made of
  **hundreds of short 1 px strokes** (8–30 px long) pointing outward, 50–90 % opacity, so
  the edge is a soft fur fringe rather than a clean line.
- **The flaw to fix:** fur strokes and a 5 px outline band are tinted 40 % toward the
  wall blue.

**`l11-figure-cutout-reference.png`** · 1600 × 2400 px, **RGBA**
- The same figure as `l11-figure-on-green.png`, rendered from the same geometry, on a
  fully **transparent** background, **without** the green fringe (edge band and strands
  in their true colors, alpha anti-aliased). This is the instructor's "catch-up" file for
  anyone whose L11 cutout fails; it can be placed straight into L12.
- Keep it in `practice/`, but hand it out only to learners who have fallen behind.

### L12 · Make It Belong

**`l12-scene-sunset.jpg`** · 2400 × 1600 px
- **Sky** (y 0–1000): vertical gradient from dusky violet-blue (`#3b3f78`) at the top
  through magenta-rose (`#c0567a`) to deep orange (`#f08a3c`) at the horizon.
- **Sun:** a disk (radius 70 px, `#ffe2a0`) centered at (1900, 960), half-sunk into the
  horizon, with a large soft radial glow (radius 500 px, warm orange, 60 % → 0 %).
- **Ground** (y 1000–1600): warm dark brown (`#4a2f22`) getting slightly lighter toward
  the horizon, with a lighter dirt path (`#8a5a3a`) as a trapezoid from bottom-center
  (width 900 px) narrowing to a point near (1200, 1000).
- **Objects with a clear light direction:** three wooden posts (dark brown rectangles):
  near post at x = 500 (height 420 px, base y = 1450), mid post at x = 1500 (220 px,
  base y = 1180), far post at x = 900 (110 px, base y = 1060). Each has a long, soft,
  dark shadow (a skewed polygon, 45 % black, blurred ~6 px) stretching **toward the lower
  left**, away from the sun. The right edge of each post has a thin warm-orange rim light.
- **Haze:** far post and far ground are lighter and lower in contrast; overall soft
  contrast (no pure black, no pure white outside the sun).
- Purpose: the default L12/L13 scene. The warm, soft light makes the cool, bright figure
  look obviously pasted.

**`l12-scene-overcast.jpg`** · 2400 × 1600 px
- Same layout and posts as the sunset scene, but: flat cool gray-blue sky (`#9aa6b2` to
  `#b8c2cc`), desaturated gray-green ground (`#5d665a`), a gray path. No sun.
- Shadows are only faint, very soft pools (15 % black, blurred ~30 px) directly under
  each post, with no direction.
- Low contrast overall. Purpose: the "same subject, two accents" solo variation and the
  soft-light half of the L13 hard-vs-soft comparison.

**`l12-subject-indoor-warm.png`** · 1600 × 2400 px
- Same figure geometry as `l11-figure-on-green.png`, on a plain mid-gray studio
  background (`#7a7a7a`) with a subtle radial vignette.
- **The flaw to fix:** a strong tungsten color cast (whole figure shifted toward orange
  `#ff9a3c`, about 30 %), high contrast, light from the **left**, and a 3 px gray edge
  band (mild fringe).
- Purpose: the solo variation's deliberately hard case, an indoor subject placed into a
  daylight scene (`l13-scene-hard-sun.jpg`). It needs brightness, a large temperature
  shift, and contrast reduction.

### L13 · Ground It & Grade It

**`l13-scene-hard-sun.jpg`** · 2400 × 1600 px
- **Sky** (y 0–700): pale blue gradient (`#6fa8e0` at top to `#cfe3f5` at the horizon).
- **Atmospheric perspective reference:** three layers of rolling hills along the horizon
  (sine-wave silhouettes): farthest `#b8cbe0` (palest, bluest, lowest contrast), middle
  `#8fa98c`, nearest `#5f8a4e`. Distant = hazier, lighter, lower contrast.
- **Ground** (y 700–1600): sunlit light tan (`#d8c39a`) with subtle noise texture.
- **Objects:** two crates (square blocks with a lighter top face and lighter **right**
  face) at (600, base 1300) size 260 px and (1700, base 1050) size 140 px, plus a thin
  pole at x = 1200 (height 500 px, base 1400).
- **Hard shadows:** each object casts a **sharp-edged, dark** shadow (60 % black, no
  blur beyond 1 px anti-aliasing) falling **toward the lower left**, with the sun implied
  high and to the right (shadow length about 0.8 × object height).
- Purpose: the hard-light half of the L13 comparison and a scene with an unmistakable
  shadow direction for the "point the shadow the wrong way, then fix it" exercise.

## Lab summaries

**L11 · A Composite-Ready Cutout (24 min).** Select ▸ Subject → Select and Mask
(Ctrl+Alt+R / ⌘⌥R) → Refine Hair, Smart Radius, Refine Edge Brush on hair, Shift Edge
slightly negative → Decontaminate Colors (raise Amount) → Output To: New Layer with Layer
Mask. Group as `SUBJECT`, keep the original hidden, save `composite-working.psd`.
**Done when** there is no fringe against a saturated Solid Color fill layer.

**L12 · Match the Subject (24 min).** File ▸ Place Embedded the scene, move it below
`SUBJECT`; convert `SUBJECT` to a Smart Object; scale with Ctrl/⌘+T (feet on the
ground). Clipped Curves (brightness) → clipped Color Balance (temperature; cool the
shadows) → clipped Curves / Hue/Saturation (contrast, ease saturation).
**Done when** every adjustment shows ↳ and the "off" state looks obviously wrong.

**L13 · Ground, Depth, Grade (32 min).** `Contact Shadow` layer below the subject (never
blurred) → `Cast Shadow` layer below that (away from the light, Gaussian Blur) → optional
haze → one grade (Color Lookup or Curves) + vignette at the top → File ▸ Save the
layered master → File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG.
**Done when** the subject stands on the ground, shadows agree with the scene, and a JPG
is exported.

**If time / homework:** match one cutout into both the sunset and overcast scenes; try
the indoor-warm hard case; add a light wrap; finish under hard vs. soft light; grade the
same composite warm and cool; add a touch of reflected light.

Do not hand out `l11-figure-cutout-reference.png` before the L11 lab. It is a catch-up
file, not a shortcut.
