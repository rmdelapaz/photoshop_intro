Module 1: Foundations

How to teach the three lessons that turn a first win into a working method: reading the layer stack, targeting an edit with a mask (black hides, white reveals), letting Photoshop find edges and baking the selection into a mask, and cleaning up a photo without destroying a pixel. This is the conceptual core of the whole course.

3 lessons 3 hrs session Lessons L02–L04 Level Beginner Maps to Session 2

How to Use This Guide

  • Same rhythm as Module 0: Frame → Demo → Guided build → Solo variation → Check. Every lesson ends with a recipe card, a checklist, and a journal prompt; use them as your closers.
  • Demo live, step by step. Demo one step, let the room do it, then move on. Name every click, and say both shortcut forms (Alt/⌥, Ctrl/⌘).
  • Teal = hands-on, amber = watch-outs. In this module the watch-outs are mostly "wrong thing selected" problems: the wrong layer, the wrong thumbnail, the wrong Sample setting. Pre-empting them saves the most time.
The #1 risk of Module 1: "it's not doing anything"

Almost every stuck learner in this module has the wrong thing selected: the photo layer instead of the mask, an empty layer with Sample All Layers unchecked, a Clone Stamp still on "Current Layer." Teach the room to diagnose before they click: "Which layer is highlighted? Which thumbnail has the border? What does the options bar say?" Write those three questions on the board.

Before Session 2

  • ☐ Copy labs/module-1/practice/ to each machine (or a shared drive). As always, never hand out the course site's licensed images/ folder.
  • ☐ Remind learners (in the Session 1 wrap-up or by email) to bring: a landscape with a pale, washed-out sky; a photo with one clear subject (person, pet, or product; hair or fur for the stretch goal); a photo with dust, a blemish, or a distracting object. Details in labs/module-1/README.md.
  • ☐ Check every machine is on Photoshop 2023 v24.5 or later (Help ▸ About Photoshop on Windows, Photoshop ▸ About Photoshop on Mac) so the Remove tool is there for L04.
  • ☐ Open lesson_02.html, lesson_03.html, and lesson_04.html in browser tabs for the before/after sliders.

The module is one story: paint a stencil by hand → let Photoshop draw the stencil → cover bad pixels with good ones on a separate sheet. The through-line is the Module 0 golden rule: never touch the original pixels.

Lesson Plans

L02

The Layer Stack & Your First Mask

50 min
Objectives
  • Read a layer stack: what's on top wins, and why order matters
  • Explain a mask in one sentence: black hides, white reveals, gray partly reveals
  • Paint on a layer mask to apply an adjustment to only part of a photo
  • Recover from mistakes by painting the mask back: no undo panic required
Timing
3mThe problem: one edit, two regions (lesson slider)
4mMental model 1: the stack of glass
6mMental model 2: a mask is a stencil
3mReading the Layers panel: two thumbnails
20mGuided build: rescue the sky (Steps 1–4)
7mNow You: spotlight a subject
7mSelf-check, recipe card, journal
Teaching sequence & live-demo script
  1. The problem (3m). Show the L02 slider. Say: "To get detail in the land, the camera blew the sky out. Darken everything and the land goes muddy. We want to darken the sky only. Notice the hills are identical on both sides."
  2. Stack of glass (4m). Upgrade the Module 0 metaphor. Say: "Layers are sheets of glass in a frame, and you look straight down. Paint on a higher sheet covers what's below; clear glass lets the lower sheets show." Write the two rules: higher layers win where they're opaque and order is meaning.
  3. A mask is a stencil (6m). Every adjustment layer comes with a layer mask, a grayscale card that decides, pixel by pixel, how much of the effect gets through. Have the whole room say the sentence aloud: "Black hides. White reveals. Gray does a bit of both." Sketch a card that's white at the top, black at the bottom, gray between. Then the superpower: Say: "Painted over something by accident? Switch to the opposite color and paint it back. Nothing is ever destroyed. That's why pros mask instead of erase."
  4. Two thumbnails (3m). Point at an adjustment layer: the effect (◐) and the mask (white card). Say: "Before you paint, click the mask thumbnail so it gets a highlighted border. On an adjustment layer your brush lands on the mask anyway, but on a pixel layer with a mask, painting with the image thumbnail selected paints the photo itself. Build the habit now."
  5. Step 1 (2m). Open l02-washed-sky.jpg (or a learner landscape). File ▸ Save As ▸ sky-rescue-working.psd. Say: "You know the rhythm: protect the original first."
  6. Step 2 (3m). ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast. Pull Brightness down until the sky looks rich. Say: "Ignore the land going too dark. The mask will fix that. Right now the mask is all white, all revealed, so the darkening hits everything."
  7. Step 3 (8m). Click the mask thumbnail. Press D (default colors), then X so black is the foreground. Edit ▸ Fill…, Contents: Black, or press Alt/⌥ + Backspace/Delete. The darkening vanishes. Say: "Not Ctrl/⌘ + Backspace: that fills with the background color, which is white, so nothing happens." Press B, X for white, pick a big soft brush (] to enlarge), and paint over the sky. Say: "Watch the mask thumbnail: a white sky-shape is growing on the black card. That's your stencil." Deliberately paint onto a hill, then press X and paint it away.
  8. Step 4 (5m). Lower brush opacity to about 30% (with the Brush active, pressing 3 sets 30%) and gently blend along the horizon. Say: "This is painting gray: a partial reveal." Toggle the eyeball for the before/after, File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → sky-rescue-final.jpg.
Lab: Rescue the Sky, then Now You

Guided build on l02-washed-sky.jpg; solo variation on l02-spotlight-object.jpg or a learner's portrait/object photo: brighten with a Brightness/Contrast layer, fill the mask black, paint white only over the subject; then a second, darkening layer masked to the background. Details in labs/module-1/README.md.

Circulate for: the mask thumbnail (not the photo) highlighted; a black mask with a white sky-shape in the thumbnail; a soft horizon, not a hard line.

Definition of done: the lesson's five-item checklist: one Brightness/Contrast layer above the photo, mask black over the land and white over the sky, a richer sky with untouched land, a soft transition, .psd saved and JPG exported.

Beginner mistakes to pre-empt
  • Painting on the photo. If the Background (photo) layer is highlighted, the brush paints real white or black paint onto the picture. Symptom: white streaks in the sky. Fix: undo, click the mask thumbnail on the adjustment layer, paint again.
  • Fill did nothing. They used Ctrl/⌘ + Backspace (background color = white). Use Alt/⌥ + Backspace with black foreground, or Edit ▸ Fill ▸ Black.
  • Colors aren't pure black and white. A previous color pick left a tinted foreground, so the reveal is partial everywhere. Press D to reset.
  • Opacity left at 30%. After softening the seam, the next strokes barely reveal anything. Press 0 for 100% (with the Brush active).
  • Hard, obvious edge. The brush Hardness is high. Right-click the canvas and lower Hardness, then paint the seam at low opacity.
  • Undo panic. Learners hammer Ctrl/⌘ + Z after every slip. Redirect: "Press X and paint it back."
Check for understanding: self-check + journal

Answers: Q1 B) It covers what's below it; Q2 A) The adjustment is hidden there; Q3 C) Ctrl/⌘ + Backspace fills with the background color (white); Q4 B) Press X and paint black over the mistake; Q5 A) Click the mask thumbnail; Q6 C) Lower brush Hardness and paint the seam at low opacity. Full set in the Answer Key.

Journal (3 min, finish at home if needed): Describe, in your own words and without looking, what happens when you paint black on a mask versus white. If you can teach it to an imaginary friend, you own it. Pair-share option: learners explain it to a neighbor first, then write.

Differentiation

Fast finishers: the lesson's "Going further": select the mask thumbnail and press Ctrl/⌘ + I to invert it and watch the reveal flip. Then try the opposite workflow from the FAQ: start with a white mask and paint black to subtract, and decide which needs less painting for this shot. Alt/⌥-clicking the mask thumbnail shows the mask itself on the canvas (click again to return), a great way to inspect a seam.

Struggling learners: skip Step 4's seam softening at first; get a black mask with a white sky first. Hand them the recipe card and the three diagnosis questions. If the black/white logic won't stick, have them paint black and white stripes on the mask and toggle the eyeball: seeing the effect switch on and off stripe by stripe usually makes it click.

L03

Selections That Hold Up

50 min
Objectives
  • Explain the difference between a selection (a temporary request) and a mask (the saved answer)
  • Select a subject automatically with Select Subject and the Object Selection tool
  • Rescue tricky edges (hair, fur, motion) in the Select & Mask workspace
  • Output a selection to a layer mask so the cutout stays non-destructive and re-editable
Timing
3mThe goal: a believable cutout (lesson slider)
5mMental model: request vs. answer
6mThe selection toolkit tour
24mGuided build: Steps 1–4 (4 + 6 + 10 + 4)
6mNow You: a wispy subject (finish during the break or at home)
6mSelf-check, recipe card, journal
Teaching sequence & live-demo script
  1. The goal (3m). Show the L03 slider. Say: "Last lesson we painted a mask by hand. Perfect for a sky, hopeless for hair or the crisp edge of a bottle. Today Photoshop finds the edge for us."
  2. Request vs. answer (5m). Make a quick selection and point at the marching ants. Say: "This outline is temporary. It's me pointing and saying 'these pixels, please.' Click elsewhere and it's gone. A mask is that request written down permanently: inside the ants becomes white, outside becomes black." Then the warning: Say: "Beginners chase a perfect selection and then delete the background, destroying pixels forever. Pros make a good-enough selection, turn it into a mask, and refine the mask forever after."
  3. Toolkit (6m). Walk the lesson's table: Select Subject (one click, AI guesses the main subject: your best first click), Object Selection (draw a loose box, it snaps to the object inside), Quick Selection (paint and it grows to matching edges), Select & Mask (a whole room for refining edges). Say: "Match the tool to the subject."
  4. Step 1 (4m). Open l03-subject-busy-bg.jpg, Save As cutout-working.psd. Select ▸ Subject. Say: "It won't be perfect. That's expected and fine." (If it grabs the wrong thing on this synthetic file, switch to the Object Selection tool and draw a box around the subject: that's also a lesson FAQ.)
  5. Step 2 (6m). Press W for Quick Selection (Shift + W cycles if Object Selection or Magic Wand appears). Paint over missed subject to add; hold Alt/⌥ and paint over included background to subtract. Say: "Get the big shape right. The fiddly edges have their own room."
  6. Step 3 (10m). Click Select and Mask… in the options bar. Set View: Overlay (masked-out areas turn a red film). Pick the Refine Edge Brush and paint gently along any wispy edge. Nudge Smooth and Feather a touch; set Shift Edge slightly negative to bite off a halo. For a colored fringe, check Decontaminate Colors in Output Settings. Say: "That switches the output to New Layer with Layer Mask. Still non-destructive: your original layer is kept, just hidden."
  7. Step 4 (4m). Set Output To: Layer Mask, click OK. Point at the new mask: "Exactly the black and white card from L02." Add a layer below: Ctrl/⌘-click the New Layer icon (or drag the new layer down), then Edit ▸ Fill… with a calm color, or place l03-calm-background.jpg there. File ▸ Save. Then the payoff: paint the mask to fix a stray, and Shift-click the mask thumbnail to turn it off and on. Say: "You never deleted a thing."
Lab: Cut Out a Subject, then Now You

Guided build on l03-subject-busy-bg.jpg (or a learner's person, pet, or product photo) placed onto l03-calm-background.jpg or a solid color. Solo variation: l03-fuzzy-subject.jpg (or a learner's hair, fur, or leafy-plant photo) using the Refine Edge Brush for the wispy parts, placed on a photo background of a different scene (l03-other-scene.jpg, a warm sunset that deliberately clashes). Details in labs/module-1/README.md.

Circulate for: Output To set to Layer Mask (or New Layer with Layer Mask), not Selection; the new background layer below the subject; no bright halo.

Definition of done: the background is hidden by a mask, not erased; the edge has no halo and isn't razor-sharp; a calmer background sits beneath; the learner can paint the mask to fix a stray; .psd saved.

Beginner mistakes to pre-empt
  • Pressing Delete. The most destructive habit in the course. If you see someone press Delete/Backspace on a selection, undo with them and say "mask it, don't erase it."
  • Output To: Selection. They click OK and get marching ants back, but no mask. Reopen Select and Mask (Select ▸ Select and Mask…, Ctrl+Alt+R / ⌘+⌥+R) and set Output To: Layer Mask.
  • "Select and Mask" button missing. It appears in the options bar only while a selection tool is active. Press W or use the Select menu.
  • "My original layer disappeared!" Decontaminate Colors makes a new masked layer and hides the original. Show them the hidden layer's eyeball: nothing was lost.
  • New background layer lands on top and covers the subject. New layers appear above the active one; Ctrl/⌘-click the New Layer icon or drag it below. If the photo is still a locked Background layer, nothing can go beneath it: click its lock icon to turn it into a normal layer first.
  • Refine Edge Brush on the solid interior. It's built for the hairline, not the middle of the subject. Paint along the edge.
Check for understanding: self-check + journal

Answers: Q1 C) A selection is temporary; a mask is the saved, editable answer; Q2 B) Use the Object Selection tool and draw a box around the thing you want; Q3 A) Hold Alt/⌥ while painting; Q4 C) The Refine Edge Brush, along the hairline; Q5 B) Layer Mask; Q6 A) Shift Edge slightly negative and Decontaminate Colors. Full set in the Answer Key.

Journal (3–5 min): Which was harder: making the selection, or making the edge believable? Tell the room: "That answer tells you where to point your practice next."

Differentiation

Fast finishers: the lesson's "Going further": select the subject, then Select ▸ Inverse (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I) to feel how a selection is just a boundary you can flip. In Select & Mask, try the Refine Hair button before brushing. Ask them to note whether the cutout's colors clash with its new background: that's the Module 4 (Compositing) question.

Struggling learners: stay on the high-contrast practice file, skip Decontaminate Colors, and aim for one thing: Output To: Layer Mask. A slightly rough edge on a real mask beats a perfect edge made by deleting pixels. Pair them with a neighbor for the Select and Mask settings.

L04

Cleaning Up: Spots, Distractions & Edges

50 min
Objectives
  • Remove small spots and blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush
  • Choose between healing (blends texture) and cloning (copies exactly), and know when each wins
  • Erase a larger distraction with Content-Aware Fill or the Remove tool
  • Work non-destructively by healing on a separate layer, then export the finished image
Timing
5mThe goal + mental model: borrow good pixels
5mWhich tool for which mess? (decision tree) + the retouch-layer habit
28mGuided build: Steps 1–4 (2 + 8 + 10 + 8)
5mNow You: start the two-ways removal (finish at home)
7mSelf-check, recipe card, journal, Module 1 recap
Teaching sequence & live-demo script
  1. Borrow good pixels (5m). Show the L04 slider. Say: "Every cleanup tool does one silly-simple thing: it takes pixels from a good part of the image and lays them over a bad part. The tools differ only in how they blend." Cloning copies exactly (great for hard edges and patterns, ruthless if lighting differs); healing copies texture and then blends color and brightness in (magic on skin and skies); Content-Aware tools do it automatically over a whole region.
  2. Decision tree + the habit (5m). Draw the tree: tiny spot → Spot Healing Brush; whole object with hard edges, straight lines, or patterns around it → Clone Stamp; whole object on plain or textured surroundings (sky, sand, grass) → Remove tool or Content-Aware Fill; then blend and check at 100%. Say: "When in doubt, try the Remove tool first. It's the most forgiving." Then the habit: all cleanup on an empty layer, each tool set to sample all layers.
  3. Step 1 (2m). Open l04-dusty-sky.jpg, Save As cleanup-working.psd. Click the New Layer icon, double-click the name and type retouch. Press J for the Spot Healing Brush and check Sample All Layers in the options bar. Say: "Everything I fix lands on this sheet. Hide it and the original is pristine."
  4. Step 2 (8m). Zoom to 100% (Ctrl/⌘ + 1). Set the brush just a little larger than a spot ([ and ] resize) and single-click it. Work left to right, top to bottom. Say: "Methodical beats fast. Don't miss any."
  5. Step 3 (10m). Option A, Remove tool (in the healing group; Photoshop 2023 v24.5 and later): still on retouch, check Sample All Layers, brush over the whole pole or bird, release. Touch up with a second pass. Option B, Content-Aware Fill: click the photo layer first (it may not be able to sample from an empty layer), loosely select the object with the Lasso (L) leaving a margin, Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill…, green-brush where it should sample from, set Output To: New Layer, OK. Say: "Now click the retouch layer again before the next step." Demo both on two different distractions so the room sees each.
  6. Step 4 (8m). Find a hard edge the healing smeared (the roofline in the practice file). Press S for the Clone Stamp and set Sample: Current & Below (or All Layers). Say: "This is a separate setting from the Spot Healing checkbox. Left on Current Layer, it samples our empty layer and paints nothing." Alt/⌥-click a clean area to sample, then paint over the flaw. Final 100% pass, File ▸ Save, File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → cleanup-final.jpg. Say: "Module 1 deliverable: done."
Lab: Clean & Export, then Now You

Guided build on l04-dusty-sky.jpg (or a learner photo with dust or a distraction). Solo variation on l04-photobomb.jpg or a learner photo with a photobomber, trash can, or power line: remove it once with the Remove tool and once with Content-Aware Fill, compare, and clean the tell-tale signs with the Clone Stamp. Start it in class; finish it at home. Details in labs/module-1/README.md.

Circulate for: a retouch layer that actually has paint on it (toggle its eyeball: the spots should come back); zoom at 100% during spot work; Clone Stamp Sample menu not on Current Layer.

Definition of done: all cleanup on a separate layer; no spots at 100%; the main distraction gone with no obvious smear or repeat; crisp edges near the repair; .psd saved and cleanup-final.jpg exported.

Beginner mistakes to pre-empt
  • Healing does nothing on the empty layer. Sample All Layers is unchecked. Check it in the options bar.
  • Clone Stamp paints nothing or warns that the area to clone hasn't been defined. Either Sample is still on Current Layer, or they never Alt/⌥-clicked a source point.
  • Retouching straight onto the photo. The photo layer was highlighted. Toggle the retouch layer's eyeball: if nothing changes, the work went onto the photo. Undo and redo on retouch.
  • Content-Aware Fill grayed out or odd. It needs an active selection, and the photo layer should be active when you open it. Afterward, click back on retouch.
  • Working zoomed out. Spots look fine at fit-to-screen and obvious at 100%. Make Ctrl/⌘ + 1 a reflex.
  • Brush far bigger than the spot drags in neighboring edges and smears. "Just a little larger than the spot."
  • Repeats and smears from content-aware tools. Check at 100%; redo with a smaller selection or clean the remnant with the Clone Stamp.
Check for understanding: self-check + journal

Answers: Q1 B) Healing blends texture into its surroundings; cloning copies pixels exactly; Q2 C) So the original is never touched and mistakes are easy to erase; Q3 A) Sample All Layers is unchecked; Q4 C) Clone Stamp; Q5 B) Set Sample to Current & Below and Alt/⌥-click a clean source; Q6 A) One pass at 100%, then view fit-to-screen. Full set in the Answer Key.

Journal (3–5 min): Look back at your Module 0 journal entry, the image you dreamed of making. Which specific skill from these three lessons gets you closest to it?

Differentiation

Fast finishers: the lesson's "Going further": combine L03 and L04 by cutting out a subject and cleaning the background behind it, a mini version of Module 4 compositing. Or try the Healing Brush (not Spot), where they Alt/⌥-click their own sample point, near an edge the Spot Healing Brush smeared.

Struggling learners: two tools only today: Spot Healing Brush for spots and the Remove tool for the pole. Skip Content-Aware Fill and the Clone Stamp; the recipe card covers them for later. The must-haves are the retouch layer and the export.

Closing the Session

Module 1 wrap-up (5 min, 2:55–3:00)

Recap the arc: read a layer stack → target an edit with a mask → make a believable selection and bake it into a mask → clean up without destroying a pixel. Exit ticket (on a sticky note): "Black ___, white ___." Homework from the lesson's "Before the Next Module": run the full Module 1 workflow on one fresh photo end to end (mask an adjustment, cut out the subject, clean the frame, export), finish the L04 solo variation, and save your best result: learners will re-grade it in Session 3. Preview Session 3 (Module 2): "Your photo is clean and controlled. Next we make it beautiful: Camera Raw, Curves, and Smart Objects."
