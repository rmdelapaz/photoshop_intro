When "it's not doing anything," ask three questions

L02

The Layer Stack & Your First Mask

50 min
By the end you can
  • Read a layer stack: what's on top wins, and why order matters
  • Explain a mask in one sentence: black hides, white reveals, gray partly reveals
  • Paint on a layer mask to apply an adjustment to only part of a photo
  • Recover from mistakes by painting the mask back: no undo panic required
Key ideas
  • The stack of glass. Layers are sheets of glass in a frame; you look straight down. Higher layers win where they're opaque. Order is meaning: move a sheet and the picture changes.
  • Every adjustment layer comes with a layer mask: a grayscale card that decides, pixel by pixel, how much of the effect gets through.
  • Black hides. White reveals. Gray does a bit of both.
  • An adjustment layer shows two thumbnails: the effect (◐) and the mask (the white/black card). Click the mask thumbnail before you paint.
  • Masks make mistakes painless: press X to swap brush color and paint the mistake back. That's why pros mask instead of erase.
Worked example: rescue the sky
  1. Open a landscape (or l02-washed-sky.jpg). File ▸ Save As ▸ sky-rescue-working.psd.
  2. Layers panel ▸ ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast. Pull Brightness down until the sky looks rich. Ignore the land going dark.
  3. Click the mask thumbnail. Press D, then X so black is the foreground. Edit ▸ Fill… ▸ Contents: Black, or Alt/⌥ + Backspace/Delete. The darkening vanishes.
  4. Press B for the Brush, X for white, pick a big soft brush, and paint over the sky. The darkening comes back only where you paint.
  5. At the horizon, lower brush opacity to about 30% and blend gently. Toggle the eyeball for the before/after. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → sky-rescue-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Mask an adjustment to "only here"
  1. Add adjustment layer (◐) → push it too far on purpose
  2. Click the mask thumbnail
  3. Fill black (hide it everywhere): Alt/⌥ + Backspace with black foreground
  4. Brush white (B, soft, big) over the area you want the effect
  5. Mistakes → press X to swap to black, paint them out

Mantra: black hides, white reveals, gray blends.

Your turn 2: Spotlight a subject

On a portrait or object photo (or l02-spotlight-object.jpg): add a Brightness/Contrast layer and brighten. Fill the mask black, then paint white only over the face or object: a gentle spotlight. Then add a second adjustment layer that darkens, masked so it only touches the background. What did the second layer do to how the subject stands out?

Going further: select a mask thumbnail and press Ctrl/⌘ + I to invert it. What happened?

Watch out

Use Alt/⌥ + Backspace to fill black, not Ctrl/⌘ + Backspace (that fills with the background color). Hard, obvious edge? Right-click the canvas, lower Hardness, and paint the seam at low opacity.

L03

Selections That Hold Up

50 min
By the end you can
  • Explain the difference between a selection (a temporary request) and a mask (the saved answer)
  • Select a subject automatically with Select Subject and the Object Selection tool
  • Rescue tricky edges (hair, fur, motion) in the Select & Mask workspace
  • Output a selection to a layer mask so your cutout stays non-destructive and re-editable
Key ideas
  • A selection shows marching ants. It's temporary: you pointing and saying "these pixels, please." Click elsewhere and it's gone.
  • A mask is the request written down permanently: inside the ants becomes white (revealed), outside becomes black (hidden).
  • Pros make a good-enough selection, turn it into a mask, and refine the mask forever after. Mask it, don't erase it.
ToolBest forHow it thinks
Select SubjectA clear main subject on any backgroundAI guesses the whole subject instantly: your best first click
Object SelectionOne object among severalDraw a loose box; it snaps to the object inside
Quick SelectionFixing or extending a selection by handPaint and it grows to matching edges
Select & MaskHair, fur, soft or motion edgesA whole room for refining the edge of any selection
Worked example: cut out a subject
  1. Open a photo with one clear subject (or l03-subject-busy-bg.jpg). Save As cutout-working.psd.
  2. Select ▸ Subject. Marching ants appear. Not perfect? Expected and fine.
  3. Quick Selection (W; Shift + W cycles the slot): paint to add missed subject; hold Alt/⌥ and paint to subtract background.
  4. Select and Mask… in the options bar. View: Overlay. Refine Edge Brush along wispy edges. Nudge Smooth and Feather; Shift Edge slightly negative. Colored fringe? Check Decontaminate Colors.
  5. Output To: Layer Mask → OK. Add a layer beneath (Ctrl/⌘-click the New Layer icon), fill it with a calm color or place another photo there. File ▸ Save.
Recipe card: Select → Mask
  1. Select ▸ Subject (let AI start)
  2. Quick Selection (W): paint to add, Alt/⌥-paint to subtract
  3. Select and Mask… → Refine Edge Brush on hair; nudge Smooth / Feather / Shift Edge (Decontaminate Colors for fringes)
  4. Output To: Layer Mask → OK
  5. New background layer beneath; paint the mask to fix strays; File ▸ Save

Mantra: mask it, don't erase it.

Your turn 1: Fix a stray, then flip the mask off

After your cutout, click the mask and paint one stray pixel away (black hides, white reveals). Then Shift-click the mask thumbnail to turn the mask off and on. What does this tell you about what happened to the background?

Your turn 2: A harder subject

Cut out something with wispy hair, fur, or leaves (or l03-fuzzy-subject.jpg). Lean on the Refine Edge Brush for the wispy parts. Place it on a photo of a different scene (or l03-other-scene.jpg). Do the colors clash? Note what you see (matching light is what Module 4 is about):

Going further: select your subject, then Select ▸ Inverse (Ctrl/⌘ + Shift + I). What's selected now?

Watch out

Never press Delete on a selection to remove a background: that destroys pixels. Always set Output To: Layer Mask. If Decontaminate Colors "hid" your original layer, it's still there: look for its eyeball.

L04

Cleaning Up: Spots, Distractions & Edges

50 min
By the end you can
  • Remove small spots and blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush
  • Choose between healing (blends texture) and cloning (copies exactly), and know when each wins
  • Erase a larger distraction with Content-Aware Fill or the Remove tool
  • Work non-destructively by healing on a separate layer, then export your finished image
Key ideas
  • Every cleanup tool borrows good pixels to cover bad ones. They differ only in how they blend.
  • Cloning copies exactly: great for hard edges and patterns, ruthless if the lighting doesn't match.
  • Healing copies texture, then blends color and brightness into the surroundings: magic on skin and skies.
  • Content-Aware tools do it automatically over a whole region.
  • The habit: clean up on an empty layer, with every tool sampling all layers: the Sample All Layers checkbox (Spot Healing Brush, Remove tool) or the Sample: menu set to Current & Below or All Layers (Healing Brush, Clone Stamp).
Worked example: clean & export
  1. Open your messy photo (or l04-dusty-sky.jpg). Save As cleanup-working.psd. Click the New Layer icon; name it retouch.
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J), check Sample All Layers. Zoom to 100% (Ctrl/⌘ + 1). Brush just a little larger than each spot; single-click it. Work left to right, top to bottom.
  3. Big distraction, Option A: Remove tool (healing group), Sample All Layers checked, brush over the whole object, release. Option B: click the photo layer, Lasso (L) a loose selection with margin, Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill…, green-brush the areas to sample from, Output To: New Layer, OK. Then click retouch again.
  4. Stubborn hard edge: Clone Stamp (S), Sample: Current & Below. Alt/⌥-click a clean area, then paint over the flaw.
  5. Final 100% pass. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → cleanup-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Clean a photo (distraction-free in five moves)
  1. New empty layer named retouch → every tool samples all layers (checkbox or Sample: menu)
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J): click each tiny spot at 100% zoom
  3. Big object → Remove tool (brush over) or Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill
  4. Stubborn edge → Clone Stamp (S, Sample: Current & Below, Alt/⌥-sample)
  5. 100% final pass → Save .psd → Export JPG

Mantra: heal to blend, clone to copy, always on a fresh layer.

Your turn 1: Prove it's non-destructive

After your spot pass, toggle the retouch layer's eyeball. What comes back? How many spots did you remove?
