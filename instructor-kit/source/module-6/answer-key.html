L17 · Generative Fill

Q1
What is the basic Generative Fill workflow?
B) Select an area, then describe what should be there
Why: The selection is the "where" and the prompt is the "what." Photoshop reads the surrounding image for context and generates content to fit the selection. There is no generative brush, and the prompt never decides placement on its own.
Q2
You want to remove a sign from the shoreline. After selecting it, what do you do?
C) Leave the prompt empty and click Generate
Why: An empty prompt tells Photoshop to fill with plausible background. Typing "sign" would ask it to generate a sign; pressing Delete leaves a hole or flat color.
Q3
Where does a Generative Fill result go?
A) On a new generative layer with its own mask
Why: The original pixels are never touched. You can hide, mask, lower the opacity of, delete, or stack another generation on the result: it is one more non-destructive layer.
Q4
Which prompt is most likely to work well?
B) "a wooden park bench"
Why: Short and concrete wins. A vague prompt gives Photoshop nothing to build; a long stylistic one fights the scene's existing light. Name the object and let Photoshop match the lighting.
Q5
Every lighthouse variation comes out short and squat. What should you change first?
C) Make a taller selection
Why: Generative Fill fills the shape you give it, so the selection's size and proportion strongly steer the result. "Selecting the right space is half the prompt."
Lab reference: Remove & Add

Correct result: generative-working.psd with the untouched original at the bottom and at least two generative layers above it: one from an empty prompt (the distraction is gone, background continues believably) and one from a described prompt (a new element that sits in the scene's light and perspective). At least one refine move is visible: a painted mask edge, a clipped Curves or Hue/Saturation, or a healing layer. At 100% there are no obvious ghosts or smears.

Common wrong results: Halo or ghost outline where the object was: the selection hugged the object; redo with a looser selection. Blurry, mushy fill: the selection was too large; generate in smaller pieces. New element too bright or wrong color: not refined; clip an adjustment to the generative layer. Only one layer in the file, or edits on the Background: learner used Content-Aware Fill or the Remove tool on the photo itself, not Generative Fill (acceptable only under the offline fallback, and then it should be on a separate layer). Garbled hands or text: an expected limit, not a learner error; use it as a teaching moment.

Offline fallback version: removal done with the Remove tool or Content-Aware Fill on a separate layer (Sample All Layers on), no "add" step. Grade it on the same checklist minus the variation and prompt items.

L18 · Generative Expand & Reframe

Q1
What is Generative Expand, really?
A) Generative Fill aimed at empty canvas around the photo
Why: Enlarging the canvas leaves empty space, and Photoshop generates scenery to continue the photo into it. Same generative layer, same variations, same optional prompt. Nothing is stretched or upsampled.
Q2
You chose Ratio 16:9 and pressed Enter, and Photoshop cropped your photo smaller. What did you skip?
C) Dragging the crop handles outward past the photo
Why: A ratio only sets the shape. Photoshop first fits that shape inside the photo, which is a normal crop. You must drag outward until the box extends past the edges (the ratio stays locked while you drag).
Q3
One big expansion came out vague and repetitive. What is the better approach?
B) Expand in smaller steps, generating each time
Why: Huge single expansions spread the generated detail thin. Several smaller expansions stay close to real pixels and look far better. A short prompt can also give the fill something specific to build.
Q4
What is the fastest giveaway of a bad expand?
B) A horizon or strong line that jumps at the seam
Why: The eye follows lines. If the horizon runs straight through, most viewers never suspect the edges were invented. Check it first, at 100%.
Q5
What does an empty prompt do in Generative Expand?
A) Continues the existing scene into the new space
Why: Just like removal in L17, an empty prompt means "fill with what belongs here." A short prompt ("open sky," "a calm beach") steers the new area instead. Filling with the background color is what Fill: Background does, not Generative Expand.
Lab reference: Uncrop & Reframe

Correct result: expand-working.psd whose canvas is larger than the original photo, with the new margins on generative layer(s) and the original pixels intact. The subject has breathing room; the document has a useful ratio (16:9, 1:1, 4:5, or 9:16); the subject's proportions are unchanged (circles stay round). At 100%, the horizon and any strong lines run continuously across the join, and a subtle clipped Curves or unifying grade makes old and new read as one image. With l18-horizon-strip.jpg the horizon should stay dead level and the diagonal fence should continue on the same angle.

Common wrong results: Photo got smaller: ratio chosen but handles not dragged outward. Flat white or black margins: Fill was left on Background. Subject looks wider or squashed: Free Transform or Image Size was used instead of Expand. Repeated clouds, trees, or rocks in the margin: one huge leap; redo in bites. Visible step in the horizon: accepted the first variation; cycle variations or regenerate a thin strip across the seam with Generative Fill. Slight brightness or color shift at the join: skipped the unifying grade.

Offline fallback version: Crop tool dragged outward with Fill: Content-Aware Fill. Expect weaker results on large expansions; grade the workflow (outward drag, bites, seam check) rather than the fill quality.

L19 · Actions, Batch & Droplets

Q1
What is an Action?
B) A recording of steps you can replay with one click
Why: You press record, do the task once, and press stop; Photoshop remembers every step. Scripts (JavaScript) are code, a separate and more advanced tool; tool presets store settings for one tool.
Q2
Why record Fit Image instead of Image Size?
C) Fit Image fits the long edge on landscape and portrait files alike
Why: Fit Image 1200 × 1200 px makes the longer side 1200 whatever the orientation. Image Size records a fixed width, so a portrait file would come out 1200 wide and far taller than intended. Record general steps that work on any file.
Q3
Every batched file was saved into the recording folder instead of the Destination. What was missed?
A) Ticking Override Action "Save As" Commands
Why: The Action contains its own Save As step. Without the override, Batch obeys the recorded save location instead of the Destination you chose.
Q4
While recording, Save As does not offer JPEG. Which step belongs before it?
C) Layer ▸ Flatten Image
Why: After Place Embedded, the file has an extra (watermark) layer. Current Photoshop only offers JPEG in Save As for a file without extra layers, so flattening must be a recorded step before saving.
Q5
sRGB web JPGs of a whole shoot, with no recording. Which tool?
B) Image Processor
Why: File ▸ Scripts ▸ Image Processor resizes and saves a folder to JPG, PSD, or TIFF in one go, optionally running an Action. Batch needs an Action; a droplet is an Action saved as an app.
Q6
What is a droplet?
A) An Action saved as a drag-and-drop app
Why: File ▸ Automate ▸ Create Droplet saves an Action as a small app. Drag files or a folder onto it and they process; it launches Photoshop itself (which must be installed).
Lab reference: Record & Batch

Correct result: an Actions panel showing Set "Export Web" containing Action "Web JPG 1200" with these steps: Convert to Profile (sRGB), Fit Image (1200 × 1200 px), Place (the watermark), Flatten Image, Save (JPEG), Close. The l19-processed/ folder holds one JPEG per input file, each with a long edge of 1200 px (landscape 1200 wide, portrait 1200 tall, square 1200 × 1200), the watermark in a consistent position, and sRGB. The l19-input/ folder is unchanged. A droplet file exists on the desktop and processes files dropped onto it.

Common wrong results: Portrait outputs 1200 wide and ~1600 tall: Image Size was recorded instead of Fit Image. Outputs missing, or in the wrong folder: Override Action "Save As" Commands not ticked. Batch stopped after a few files: a warning dialog (missing profile) halted it; tick Suppress color profile warnings and log errors to a file. Watermark in a different spot on portrait files: position depends on canvas size; acceptable for this lab if it is fully on the canvas, a good discussion of "record on a typical image, test on an odd one." Small file enlarged to 1200 and soft (l19-batch-07.jpg): Fit Image enlarges by default; point out the Don't Enlarge option in the Fit Image dialog. Extra steps like "Select" or "Move" in the Action: the learner kept recording while exploring; delete those steps. Originals overwritten: Destination pointed at the input folder; restore from the practice folder and discuss why copies matter.
