L05

The Develop Step: Camera Raw

42 min
By the end you can
  • Open a photo as a Smart Object so Camera Raw becomes a re-editable Smart Filter
  • Fix a color cast with White Balance (Temperature and Tint)
  • Set global tone with Exposure, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Whites and Blacks
  • Explain why you develop globally first, before any local edits
Key ideas
  • Camera Raw is the develop step: the digital darkroom where you get the raw light honest before any creative choice.
  • Global before local. Fix the whole image (white balance, overall tone) first. Dodging and burning a photo that still has an orange cast is just polishing a problem.
  • Convert the layer to a Smart Object first. Then Camera Raw attaches as a Smart Filter you can double-click and re-edit forever.
  • Work the panel top to bottom: White Balance, then Exposure → Contrast → Highlights/Shadows → Whites/Blacks.
  • Clipping means a highlight has gone pure white (or a shadow pure black) with no detail left. Hold Alt / ⌥ while dragging Whites or Blacks to see exactly where it happens.
Worked example: the develop pass
  1. File ▸ Open your photo, then File ▸ Save As → grade-working.psd.
  2. Right-click the layer on its name (not the thumbnail) ▸ Convert to Smart Object. Look for the page badge on the thumbnail.
  3. Filter ▸ Camera Raw Filter… (Shift + Ctrl + A / ⇧ + ⌘ + A).
  4. White Balance eyedropper: click something that should be neutral gray or white. Fine-tune Temperature and Tint. Look away, then look back.
  5. Exposure first, a gentle Contrast nudge, Highlights down, Shadows up.
  6. Hold Alt / ⌥: drag Whites until just shy of clipping, Blacks down until you have a true black.
  7. OK. Toggle the Smart Filter's 👁 for before/after. File ▸ Save (Ctrl/⌘ + S).

Your screen may group the sliders a little differently from this list (section names vary between Camera Raw versions). Follow the order, wherever the sliders sit.

L06

Curves: Precise Tone & Color

50 min
By the end you can
  • Read the Curves graph: input across, output up, the diagonal is "no change"
  • Add contrast with an S-curve, and flatten it for a soft, filmic look
  • Color-grade by bending the Red, Green and Blue channels
  • Apply a Curves adjustment layer to only part of the photo with a mask
Key ideas
  • A curve is a tone translator. Tones walk in along the bottom (input) and out up the side (output). The straight diagonal means "leave it alone."
  • Bend a point up and those tones get brighter; bend it down and they get darker. You choose which tones: shadows, midtones, or highlights.
  • S-curve = shadows down, highlights up = more contrast. Lifted bottom-left corner = milky, faded blacks (matte, vintage).
  • Channel curves grade color. In the Blue channel, shadows up + highlights down = cool shadows, warm highlights. Add Red shadows down for a truer teal. Move in tiny amounts.
  • A masked Curves layer grades one region only: fill the mask black, paint white where you want the effect.
  • Curves layers go above your Camera Raw develop. Global first, again.
Worked example: contrast & color
  1. Open grade-working.psd from L05. Click ◐ at the bottom of the Layers panel ▸ Curves.
  2. Click a point about a quarter of the way up the line and drag it gently down. Click about three quarters up and drag gently up. That's your S.
  3. Change the channel menu from RGB to Blue: lift the shadow end slightly, drop the highlight end slightly. For truer teal, switch to Red and pull its shadow end down slightly.
  4. Add a second Curves layer and brighten it noticeably.
  5. Click its mask thumbnail. Press D, then X so black is the foreground, then Alt + Backspace / ⌥ + Delete to fill the mask black (or press Ctrl/⌘ + I to invert the white mask).
  6. Press X for white; paint over your subject with a soft brush (B). File ▸ Save.

Watch out

Colors went wild? You moved a channel too far: a few pixels of drag on Blue is already a strong grade. Paint showing up as white smears on the photo? You had the image selected, not the mask. Undo and click the mask thumbnail first. Skin blotchy or sky banded? Your S-curve is too strong for that area; soften it or mask it.

L07

Smart Objects & Finishing the Grade

45 min
By the end you can
  • Explain what a Smart Object is: a protective container around your pixels
  • Scale, warp, and transform without losing quality
  • Apply Smart Filters that stay re-editable and maskable
  • Finish a fully non-destructive grade and export it
Key ideas
  • A Smart Object stores your pixels; everything you do to it is a re-editable instruction, not a permanent change.
  • Scale a normal layer down and back up and it turns to mush: the pixels were thrown away. A Smart Object re-renders crisp from the sealed original.
  • Any filter on a Smart Object nests beneath it as a Smart Filter. Filters share one filter mask, the newest sits on top, and they apply from the bottom up.
  • Paint the filter mask black to hide the filter in an area (black hides, white reveals).
  • Don't heal or clone directly on a Smart Object: those tools want normal pixels. Retouch first, then convert.
  • Keep the layered .psd as your master; export a flat JPEG as the deliverable.
Worked example: finish the grade
  1. Open grade-working.psd. Check the photo layer's thumbnail for the page-fold badge (if missing: right-click ▸ Convert to Smart Object).
  2. With the Smart Object selected: Filter ▸ Sharpen ▸ Smart Sharpen (crisp detail) or Filter ▸ Blur ▸ Gaussian Blur (a dreamy glow).
  3. For a glow: double-click the small Blending Options icon at the right end of the Gaussian Blur row, set Mode: Soft Light (or Screen), Opacity about 20–40%.
  4. Click the white Smart Filters mask and paint black where you don't want the filter.
  5. Ctrl/⌘ + T: scale to 30%, commit; scale back to 100%, commit. Still crisp? (If Photoshop warns that Smart Filters are off while transforming, click OK.)
  6. File ▸ Save the .psd. Then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → module2-grade-final.jpg.
