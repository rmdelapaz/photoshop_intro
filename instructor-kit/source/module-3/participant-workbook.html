L08

Natural Skin: Heal, Don't Erase

37 min
By the end you can
  • Retouch skin non-destructively on a separate empty layer
  • Remove temporary blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush and Healing Brush
  • Decide what to fix and what to leave
  • Dial back your work with layer opacity so it never looks overdone
Key ideas
  • Great retouching is invisible. The person should look like themselves on a good day, not like a wax figure.
  • Ask of every "flaw": would it be there next week? No → temporary (spot, stray hair, shine, lint): heal it. Yes → permanent (freckles, moles, laugh lines, scars, nose shape): keep it, or reduce it, never erase.
  • Survey before tools. Zoom to 100% and sort what you see before touching anything.
  • All healing goes on an empty retouch layer. Spot Healing Brush: tick Sample All Layers. Healing Brush: set Sample: Current & Below. They're separate settings.
  • Spot Healing is one-click and picks its own source (open skin). The Healing Brush lets you choose the source with Alt/⌥-click (edges, hairlines).
  • Lower the retouch layer to about 85–90% opacity so a whisper of the original keeps it believable.
Worked example: clean skin
  1. Open the portrait. File ▸ Save As → portrait-working.psd.
  2. Zoom to 100% (Ctrl/⌘ + 1) and pan slowly. Sort what you see: temporary or permanent.
  3. New empty layer, name it retouch, and keep it selected.
  4. Spot Healing Brush (J): Type: Content-Aware, Sample All Layers ✓. Brush just larger than each blemish; single-click.
  5. Near an edge (hairline, eyebrow): Healing Brush (Shift + J cycles the group), Sample: Current & Below. Alt/⌥-click clean skin of the same brightness, then click the blemish. Still smearing? Finish with the Clone Stamp (S), same Sample setting.
  6. Retouch layer Opacity about 85–90%. Toggle its 👁 to compare. Save.

Your turn: Retouch checklist
  • ☐ All healing is on the separate retouch layer (tools set to sample the layers below)
  • ☐ Temporary blemishes are gone
  • ☐ Freckles, moles, and lines are still there
  • ☐ Retouch layer opacity is slightly under 100%
  • ☐ With the retouch layer on, it still looks like the same person
Watch out

Brush does nothing? Check the options bar: Sample All Layers (Spot Healing) or Sample: Current & Below (Healing Brush). Color patch after a heal? It sampled a shadow or highlight; sample skin of the same brightness. Tempted to remove every freckle? Don't. That's what makes a face look plastic.

L09

Frequency Separation

55 min
By the end you can
  • Explain high frequency (texture) vs low frequency (color and tone)
  • Build the setup with Gaussian Blur + Apply Image
  • Even out blotchy skin tone on the color layer, texture untouched
  • Fix stray texture on the texture layer, color untouched
Key ideas
  • Every image is two things layered together. Low frequency: the slow, soft stuff (overall color, shading, blotches): the paint. High frequency: the fast, fine stuff (pores, fine hairs, crisp edges): the texture stamped into it.
  • Blurring a face smooths both at once, which is why it looks plastic. Frequency separation puts them on two layers so you can fix one without harming the other.
  • LOW = a blurred copy (Normal mode). HIGH = the texture extracted with Apply Image, in Linear Light mode, on top. Together they look identical to the original.
  • Color on LOW, texture on HIGH: never mix them.
  • The Apply Image recipe depends on bit depth (Image ▸ Mode). 8-bit: Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128. 16-bit: Add, Scale 2, Offset 0, Invert.
Worked example: the recipe card
  1. Open your portrait from L08. Stamp Visible: Ctrl + Alt + Shift + E / ⌘ + ⌥ + ⇧ + E.
  2. Duplicate it twice (Ctrl/⌘ + J ×2). Lower copy = LOW, upper copy = HIGH. Hide HIGH.
  3. Select LOW: Filter ▸ Blur ▸ Gaussian Blur until blotches are gone and pores just disappear (often ~6–12 px).
  4. Show and select HIGH: Image ▸ Apply Image: Layer LOW, Blending Subtract, Scale 2, Offset 128 (8-bit). OK. HIGH turns flat gray.
  5. Set HIGH's blend mode to Linear Light. The image looks normal again. ✅ Checkpoint.
  6. On LOW: Lasso a blotchy area, Select ▸ Modify ▸ Feather 10–20 px, small Gaussian Blur. Or paint with a soft brush at low opacity, sampling good nearby color.
  7. On HIGH: Healing Brush or Clone Stamp with Sample: Current Layer to remove a flyaway hair. Save.

L10

Dodge & Burn: Sculpting Light

45 min
By the end you can
  • Sculpt a face by dodging (lightening) and burning (darkening)
  • Do it non-destructively on a 50% gray Soft Light layer
  • Add subtle life to eyes and teeth with masked adjustments
  • Finish and export a complete, natural portrait retouch
Key ideas
  • Dodge = lighten, burn = darken. You're a lighting artist adding highlights and shadows after the shot.
  • Faces look 3D because the brow, nose bridge, cheekbones and chin catch light, and the sides, temples, eye sockets and under the jaw fall into shadow.
  • On a 50% gray layer in Soft Light mode, gray is invisible. Paint white to dodge, black to burn. Non-destructive and re-editable.
  • ~10% brush opacity, many passes. You can always add more; you can't un-see a harsh streak.
  • Follow the existing light. If light comes from the left, dodge the left-facing planes and burn the right.
  • Finish with a slightly brightened, masked Curves layer on the eye whites and teeth: clean, not glowing.
Worked example: sculpt & finish
  1. Alt/⌥-click the New Layer button. Mode: Soft Light, tick Fill with Soft-Light-neutral color (50% gray). Name it D&B. It looks invisible: correct.
  2. Brush (B), soft and large, Opacity ~10%. Press D, then X so white is the foreground. Dodge the catch-light zones with repeated light strokes.
  3. Press X for black. Burn the shadow zones.
  4. Add a Curves layer, brighten slightly, fill its mask black, paint white over the eye whites and teeth. (Optional: a second tiny Curves layer masked to the irises for a little sparkle.)
  5. Fit to screen. File ▸ Save the .psd, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As (JPG, about 80%) → portrait-final.jpg.

Want to see your light "map"? Alt/⌥-click the D&B layer's 👁 to show it alone, then Alt/⌥-click again to return.
