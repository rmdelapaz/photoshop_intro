L01 · Fix & Export One Photo

Q1
What does non-destructive editing mean?
B) Making changes on layers above the photo, so the original pixels are never altered
Why: The tracing-paper idea: changes live on sheets (layers) you can toggle, tweak, or peel off. A) confuses "non-destructive" with "subtle"; a big edit on an adjustment layer is still non-destructive. C) is still editing pixels directly, just on a copy.
Q2
Why do you choose File ▸ Save As and the .psd format right after opening your photo?
B) A .psd remembers your layers so you can change your mind, and your original JPEG stays untouched
Why: This is the tracing-paper principle applied to the file itself. A .psd is usually larger than a JPEG (A is wrong), and Photoshop opens JPEGs fine (C is wrong); a JPEG simply can't store layers.
Q3
With the Crop tool active, how do you straighten a tilted horizon?
A) Move the cursor just outside a corner until it becomes a bent arrow, then drag to rotate until the grid lines match the horizon
Why: Straightening is rotating the crop grid to agree with a line that should be level. Dragging a corner inward (B) only trims edges; dragging inside the box (C) repositions the image.
Q4
You click the eyeball next to your Brightness/Contrast layer and the whole effect disappears. What does that tell you?
C) The edit lives on the adjustment layer, not in the photo itself
Why: This is "the magic moment" from the lesson: non-destructive editing you can see. Nothing failed (A), and the photo was never permanently changed (B).
Q5
What's the difference between Save and Export?
B) Save writes your layered .psd so you can keep editing; Export flattens everything into a share-ready .jpg
Why: You do both, for different reasons, every time: the .psd is the editable master, the .jpg is the copy you share. Never post the .psd; never edit on the .jpg.
Q6
You can't find the Layers panel anywhere on screen. What do you do?
A) Choose Window ▸ Layers (or press F7)
Why: Every panel lives in the Window menu. (Resetting the workspace with Window ▸ Workspace also brings panels back, but the direct fix is Window ▸ Layers.)
Lab reference: guided build (l01-tilted-horizon.jpg or own photo)

A correct result looks like: two files. myphoto-working.psd opens with two layers: the photo at the bottom and a Brightness/Contrast adjustment layer above it; toggling that layer's eyeball shows a clear before/after. myphoto-final.jpg is cropped (the dark blob in the practice file's bottom-left corner is gone), the horizon is level (within about half a degree to the eye), and shadows are more open with a little more punch. The original .jpg is unchanged on disk.

Common wrong results and why:

  • Only one layer in the .psd, but the photo is brighter. The learner used Image ▸ Adjustments (a direct, destructive edit) instead of the ◐ button in the Layers panel. Show them the ◐ button and redo Step 3.
  • Horizon tilted the other way, or still tilted. They rotated in the wrong direction or estimated by eye without lining a grid line up with the horizon. Have them rotate until one horizontal grid line sits exactly on it.
  • Crop is a perfect square or odd shape. The Crop tool's ratio menu was set from a previous use. Click Clear in the options bar and redo.
  • No .psd anywhere, or the .psd has no adjustment layer. They skipped Save As, or skipped the final File ▸ Save before exporting, so the .psd only holds the state from Step 1.
  • Original photo was overwritten. They exported with the original's name into the same folder. Lesson learned: always export as -final.jpg.
  • Washed-out, flat-gray result. Brightness pushed very high with no Contrast. "Nudge" both; this file needs a moderate lift, not the maximum.
Lab reference: independent pass (learner's own photo, or l01-too-bright.jpg)

Own photos vary, so judge the process, not a specific look: a .psd saved before editing, at least one adjustment layer that toggles cleanly, a new-name export, and an original left untouched. With the lesson's too-bright twist, a correct result looks like: a .psd with two adjustment layers above the photo (Brightness/Contrast with Brightness lowered slightly, and Vibrance with Vibrance raised gently), plus an exported .jpg. Colors look richer without neon edges; highlights have a little more detail than the original.

Common wrong results and why:

  • Garish, neon colors. Saturation was pushed instead of (or as well as) Vibrance, or Vibrance went to the maximum. Vibrance is the gentler control; "gently" is the lesson's word.
  • Only one adjustment layer. They edited the existing Brightness/Contrast layer again instead of clicking ◐ a second time. The point of the exercise is seeing two sheets of tracing paper.
  • A visibly worse result than the original. Usually a dark photo darkened further or a bright one brightened: the learner copied the demo's slider direction instead of looking at their own photo. Ask "what does this photo need?" and let them fix it on the same layer (that's the point of adjustment layers).
  • "Reordering changed nothing." A valid observation: with these two adjustments the difference is often subtle. The going-further question is about curiosity, not a specific answer.
