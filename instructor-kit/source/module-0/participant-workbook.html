How This Course Works

Project-driven, not a tool tour

Every module ends in a finished deliverable, and you learn each skill at the moment the project needs it. Eight modules, one per session: 0 First Win · 1 Foundations · 2 Light & Color · 3 Retouching · 4 Compositing · 5 Design & Type · 6 AI & Speed · 7 Capstone (a portfolio piece you choose and present).

It spirals

Four big ideas keep coming back, each time a little deeper: non-destructive editing, the layer stack, masks as grayscale, and selections. You don't "finish" them today. You keep meeting them until they're second nature.

The Recipes library

Short, standalone how-tos ("How do I straighten a crooked horizon?") you can use in any order, each linked to the lesson that teaches the why. Every lesson also ends with a recipe card, reprinted in this workbook. You don't have to memorize everything.

A word before you start

If Photoshop feels like "too much," that feeling is normal, and temporary. You are not going to learn all of Photoshop. Nobody has. You're going to learn the handful of ideas it's built on, and then use them. Add the word yet to anything that feels hard today: "I can't straighten a horizon yet."

L01

Fix & Export One Photo

guided + independent pass
By the end you can
  • Open a photo and straighten & crop it with the Crop tool
  • Improve its brightness and contrast with a Brightness/Contrast adjustment layer
  • Explain, in one sentence, what non-destructive editing means and why it matters
  • Export a finished JPEG without ever altering your original file
Key ideas
  • Tracing paper. Instead of drawing on the photo with a marker, you lay clear sheets over it and make your changes there. Hate a change? Peel that sheet off. The photo underneath is never touched.
  • In Photoshop those sheets are layers. Special ones that only carry an instruction ("make everything under me brighter") are adjustment layers.
  • Non-destructive editing = working so the original is always intact. It's the actual professional workflow, not a beginner shortcut.
  • Four places on screen today: Tools (left edge), Options bar (along the top), Canvas (the big middle), Layers panel (bottom-right). Missing the Layers panel? Window ▸ Layers or F7.
  • Save vs. Export. Save writes your layered working file (.psd) so you can keep editing. Export flattens everything into one share-ready image (.jpg). Never post the .psd; never edit on the .jpg.
Worked example: the guided build
  1. Open & save a working file. File ▸ Open your photo. Immediately File ▸ Save As, format Photoshop (.psd), name it myphoto-working.psd. (If asked where, choose Save on your computer.)
  2. Straighten & crop. Press C for the Crop tool. Drag a corner inward to remove distracting edges. To straighten, move the cursor just outside a corner until it becomes a bent arrow, then drag to rotate until the grid lines match the horizon. Press Enter (Return).
  3. Add ONE adjustment layer. At the bottom of the Layers panel click ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast. Nudge Brightness up until the shadows open; nudge Contrast up a little. Toggle the eyeball next to the new layer off and on: the edit vanishes and returns.
  4. Save, then export. File ▸ Save (Ctrl/⌘ + S). Then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As…, format JPG, quality about 80%, and save as myphoto-final.jpg.
Recipe card: Quick Fix & Export (the 5-step loop)
  1. Open → Save As .psd (protect the original)
  2. C Crop & straighten → Enter
  3. Layers panel ▸ ◐ ▸ Brightness/Contrast → nudge
  4. (Optional) ◐ ▸ Vibrance → nudge
  5. File ▸ Save, then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80%

Golden rule: edits live on adjustment layers, never on the photo.

Your turn 1: The magic moment

After Step 3, click the eyeball next to your adjustment layer off and on three times. In your own words, what does this prove about your original photo?

Your turn 2: Independent pass on your own photo

Now do the whole loop again, alone, on a photo of your own. The lesson's twist: pick one that's too bright (or use l01-too-bright.jpg). Run the same four steps, but lower the brightness a touch. Then click ◐ again and add a Vibrance layer; push Vibrance up gently. Export and compare it with the original. Name your files after your subject (for example beach-working.psd and beach-final.jpg).

Share-out: one choice I made

You'll show your before/after by toggling the eyeballs. Finish this sentence to say out loud: "I ______ because ______." (Example: "I lowered brightness because the sky was blown out.")

Watch out

Save As .psd first, every time. It protects your original. Export with a new name (-final.jpg) so you never overwrite the photo you started with. If the crop box snaps to a fixed shape, click Clear in the options bar.

Self-check
Q1
What does non-destructive editing mean?
A) Only making small, subtle changes so nothing looks overdone
B) Making changes on layers above the photo, so the original pixels are never altered
C) Editing a JPEG copy and deleting it if you don't like it
Q2
Why do you choose File ▸ Save As and the .psd format right after opening your photo?
A) A .psd is smaller, so it's faster to share
B) A .psd remembers your layers so you can change your mind, and your original JPEG stays untouched
C) Photoshop can't open or edit JPEGs at all
Q3
With the Crop tool active, how do you straighten a tilted horizon?
A) Move the cursor just outside a corner until it becomes a bent arrow, then drag to rotate until the grid lines match the horizon
B) Drag a corner handle inward until the horizon looks level
C) Click and drag inside the crop box to slide the photo
Q4
You click the eyeball next to your Brightness/Contrast layer and the whole effect disappears. What does that tell you?
A) The adjustment failed and needs to be redone
B) The photo was permanently brightened and is now being hidden
C) The edit lives on the adjustment layer, not in the photo itself
Q5
What's the difference between Save and Export?
A) There's no difference; they're two names for the same command
B) Save writes your layered .psd so you can keep editing; Export flattens everything into a share-ready .jpg
C) Save is for Windows and Export is for Mac
Q6
You can't find the Layers panel anywhere on screen. What do you do?
A) Choose Window ▸ Layers (or press F7)
B) Reinstall Photoshop
C) Close the photo and open it again
