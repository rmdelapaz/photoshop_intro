L11

Cut It Out Cleanly

The new background reveals every flaw the old one hid.

Mental model

The background tells on you

  • Edge pixels picked up color from what was behind the subject
  • Shot on green grass? The hair holds a faint green
  • On a sunset, that fringe screams "pasted"
  • So: choose the right pixels and clean the color out of the edge
Select & Mask, tuned for compositing

Composite-ready in five moves

  1. Select ▸ Subject, then Select and Mask… CtrlAltR / ⌘⌥R
  2. Refine Hair + Smart Radius; Refine Edge Brush on hair; Shift Edge slightly negative
  3. Output Settings: tick Decontaminate Colors, raise Amount
  4. Output To: New Layer with Layer Mask
  5. Test against a saturated color; keep the original as a backup
Watch-outs

Hair only. Smooth & Feather near 0.

  • Refine Edge Brush on a jaw or shoulder = mush
  • It rebuilds strands; it can't invent hair you never selected
  • Decontamination rewrites pixels, so it forces a new layer
DONE WHEN no fringe against a wild color · group named SUBJECT · original hidden below
L12

Make It Belong

Teach the subject the scene's accent.

One rule beats a hundred sliders

Brightness → Color → Contrast

  1. Brightness: as bright as its neighbors? (squint)
  2. Color: the scene's temperature, on the lit side
  3. Contrast: soft and hazy, or punchy and crisp?
TIP brightness alone is often 80% of the "pasted" feeling
Clip every adjustment

Affect the subject, never the scene

↳ Hue/Saturation
↳ Color Balance
↳ Curves
SUBJECT (Smart Object)
Background scene
  • Clip: CtrlAltG / ⌘⌥G
  • or Alt/⌥-click the line between layers
  • Look for the ↳ and the underlined name
WATCH sky changed color? You forgot to clip.
Warm light, cool shadows

Don't dip it in orange

  • Convert SUBJECT to a Smart Object before you scale; feet on the ground
  • Warm the lit side; a touch cooler in the shadows
  • Pasted subjects are often too saturated: ease it down
TEST squint, then flick the clipped layers off and on: "off" should look wrong
L13

Ground It & Grade It

Nothing real floats.

Three cues, three scales

Shadow · Depth · Unity

  • Shadow (the object): contact + cast shadow
  • Depth (the space): distant = hazier, lighter, lower contrast
  • Unity (the whole): one grade over everything
Order is the trick

The finishing stack

Unifying Grade (over ALL)
Atmosphere / haze
▸ SUBJECT
Contact Shadow (never blurred)
Cast Shadow (blurred)
Background scene
  • Contact: tight, darkest at the feet
  • Cast: away from the light, softer as it travels
  • Grade: Color Lookup or gentle Curves + soft vignette
WATCH one sun, one shadow direction: sun high-right → shadow low-left
Ship it

Save the master, then export

  • File ▸ Save the layered .psd (don't flatten)
  • File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG (flattened automatically)
  • Final squint test
DONE WHEN contact + cast shadow · subtle haze · one grade on top · JPG exported 🏆
