Module 1 • Lesson 3🧽 Cleaning Up: Spots, Distractions & Edges

This is the finale of Module 1. You'll make the little annoyances vanish — dust spots, a blemish, that one distracting object at the edge of the frame — and export a finished, cleaned-up photo. The secret is almost silly: you cover the bad pixels with good ones borrowed from nearby.

📚 What You'll Learn

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Remove small spots and blemishes with the Spot Healing Brush
  • Choose between healing (blends texture) and cloning (copies exactly), and know when each wins
  • Erase a larger distraction with Content-Aware Fill or the Remove tool
  • Work non-destructively by healing on a separate layer, then export your finished image

⏱️ Estimated Time: 50 minutes

🎯 Project: Take the messy photo you've been building on and deliver it clean: spotless, distraction-free, and exported.

🧼 The Goal: A Clean Frame

🧠 Mental Model: Borrow Good Pixels to Cover Bad Ones

Every cleanup tool in Photoshop does the same simple thing: it takes pixels from a good part of the image and lays them over a bad part. The tools differ only in how they blend what they borrow:

  • Cloning copies the borrowed pixels exactly: same color, same brightness. Great for hard edges and patterns; ruthless if the lighting doesn't match.
  • Healing copies the borrowed texture but then blends its color and brightness into the surroundings. Magic on skin and skies; it makes the repair invisible.
  • Content-Aware tools do this automatically over a whole region. Photoshop looks around, invents a plausible fill, and blends it in.

🧠 The one habit that saves you every time

Do all your cleanup on a separate, empty layer with each tool set to sample all layers (a Sample All Layers checkbox on the Spot Healing Brush and Remove tool; a Sample: menu set to Current & Below or All Layers on the Healing Brush and Clone Stamp). If a heal goes wrong, you erase it off that layer. The original photo underneath is never touched. It's the tracing-paper principle from Module 0, applied to retouching.

🛠️ Guided Build: Clean & Export

Open your messy photo and File ▸ Save As ▸ cleanup-working.psd.

Step 1: Make a retouch layer · 2 min

  1. Click the New Layer icon in the Layers panel. Name it retouch.
  2. Pick the Spot Healing Brush (J) and in the options bar check Sample All Layers.
💡 Why the empty layer: everything you fix lands on retouch, not the photo. Hide or erase that layer and your original is pristine: non-destructive retouching.

Step 2: Zap the spots · 8 min

  1. Zoom to 100% (Ctrl/⌘ + 1) and scan across the sky/skin for dust spots and blemishes.
  2. Set your brush just a little larger than each spot, and single-click it. The Spot Healing Brush samples nearby texture and blends automatically. 🎉
  3. Work methodically left-to-right, top-to-bottom, so you don't miss any.

Step 3: Remove the big distraction · 10 min

Option A, the Remove tool (Photoshop 2023 v24.5 and later):

  1. Pick Remove (in the healing group), check Sample All Layers in the options bar (you're still on the empty retouch layer), and simply brush over the whole object.
  2. Release. Photoshop fills it in with plausible background. Touch up any leftover with a second pass.

Option B, Content-Aware Fill:

  1. Click the photo layer in the Layers panel so Content-Aware Fill has real pixels to sample (depending on your version, it may not be able to sample from an empty layer).
  2. Loosely select the object (Lasso tool, L), leaving a little margin.
  3. Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill… A workspace opens with a live preview.
  4. Green-brush the areas Photoshop should sample from; set Output To: New Layer (so the photo stays untouched); click OK. Then click your retouch layer again before Step 4.

⚠️ Watch for smears and repeats

Content-aware tools sometimes clone a recognizable shape twice, or smear a soft blob. Check at 100%. If it repeats, redo it with a smaller selection or clean up the remnant with the Clone Stamp.

Step 4: Clone for the stubborn bits, then export · 8 min

  1. For a hard edge the healing tools smeared, grab the Clone Stamp (S) and set Sample: Current & Below (or All Layers) in the options bar; it's a separate setting from the Spot Healing Brush, and on the default Current Layer it would sample your empty layer and paint nothing. Alt/⌥-click a clean area to sample, then paint over the flaw. It copies exactly, keeping edges crisp.
  2. Do a final 100% pass across the whole image.
  3. File ▸ Save (your layered .psd), then File ▸ Export ▸ Export As ▸ JPG 80% → cleanup-final.jpg. Module 1 deliverable: done. 🏆

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ All cleanup lives on a separate retouch (or Content-Aware) layer
  • ☐ Dust spots and blemishes are gone at 100% zoom
  • ☐ The main distraction is removed with no obvious smear or repeat
  • ☐ Edges near the repair are still crisp
  • ☐ I saved the .psd and exported a finished .jpg

🍳 Recipe Card: Clean a Photo

Distraction-free in five moves

  1. New empty layer named retouch → every tool samples all layers (checkbox or Sample: menu)
  2. Spot Healing Brush (J): click each tiny spot at 100% zoom
  3. Big object → Remove tool (brush over) or Edit ▸ Content-Aware Fill
  4. Stubborn edge → Clone Stamp (S, Sample: Current & Below, Alt/⌥-sample)
  5. 100% final pass → Save .psd → Export JPG

Mantra: heal to blend, clone to copy, always on a fresh layer.

❓ Common Questions at This Stage

My heal looks blurry or smeared. Why?

The Spot Healing Brush blends with whatever's nearby. If that includes a hard edge or a different color, it smears. Near edges, switch to the Clone Stamp (copies exactly) or the Healing Brush where you pick the sample point yourself. (On a retouch layer, set either tool's Sample: menu to Current & Below first.)

Content-Aware Fill invented something weird.

It samples from the surrounding area by default. In its workspace, paint the green "sampling" overlay only over the clean areas you want it to copy from, and it'll stop borrowing the weird stuff.

How do I know when a photo is "clean enough"?

Do one pass at 100% zoom, then step back and view it fit-to-screen. If nothing pulls your eye away from the subject, you're done. Perfect at 400% that no one will ever see is wasted effort.
