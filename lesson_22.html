Module 7 • Lesson 3🖼️ Present & Ship

The final lesson. Your capstone exists; now you make it land. Presentation is not decoration, it is the difference between "here is a file" and "here is a piece." This lesson gets your work seen well, critiqued honestly, and out into the world, and then we celebrate how far you have come.

  • Build a portfolio entry and know where to grow next

🧠 Mental Model: Present, Critique, Ship

Finishing the pixels is not the finish line. Three more moves turn a file into a piece people respond to:

  • Present. Show the work where it would live: a poster on a wall, an app on a phone, a photo in a frame. Context tells the viewer how to read it.
  • Critique. Judge it honestly against a rubric before anyone else does. Self-critique is how you improve faster than time alone would allow.
  • Ship. Actually put it out: portfolio, socials, a print on your wall. Work that stays on your drive teaches you far less than work that meets an audience.

🧠 Done is a decision

No piece is ever perfect; you decide it is finished. The rubric ahead helps you make that call with confidence instead of endless fiddling. Shipping a good piece today beats polishing a slightly better one forever.

🛠️ Guided Build: Present Your Work

Step 1: Export for the destination · 5 min

  1. Keep your layered .psd master. Export a delivery copy: JPG or PNG in sRGB for screen, or a print PDF at 300 ppi, in CMYK, with bleed, to the printer's spec (Module 5's export rules).
  2. Name it clearly and size it for where it is going.

Step 2: Present it in context · 12 min

  1. Place your export into a mockup: a frame on a wall, a phone or laptop screen, a poster on a billboard. Mockup templates are layered PSD files (Adobe Stock and many design sites offer them) built around a Smart Object (Module 2).
  2. To use one, double-click the Smart Object layer's thumbnail. Its contents open in their own tab (a .psb file). Place or paste your art there, scale it to fill, then save (Ctrl/⌘ + S) and close that tab. Back in the mockup, your art appears warped to fit the scene.
  3. Add a soft shadow and a believable setting. Suddenly it looks like a product, not homework.

✅ Show your process too

Pair the final with a before/after or a couple of process snapshots from 7.2. Viewers (and clients, and hiring managers) love seeing the journey, and it proves the skill is yours.

Step 3: Write a two-sentence statement · 8 min

  1. Sentence one: what it is and who it is for. Sentence two: one interesting decision you made and why.
  2. Example: "A gig poster for a local retro band. I graded it warm and added film grain so it feels like a memory of a summer night."

✅ Project Completion Checklist

  • ☐ Delivery file exported correctly for its destination
  • ☐ Presented in at least one context mockup with a shadow
  • ☐ A before/after or process shot accompanies it
  • ☐ A two-sentence artist statement written
  • ☐ The layered master is saved and safe

🔎 The Honest Critique

Before you ship, run your piece through this rubric. Be the tough critic now, so the internet does not have to be:

AskLooking for
Focal pointDoes one thing clearly win my attention first?
Light & colorIs it consistent across the whole image? Any element that does not belong?
Edges & masksAny fringe, halo, or chopped hair at 100%?
Type (if any)Clear hierarchy, tasteful spacing, one or two fonts?
BelievabilityDo shadows agree on one light? Any telltale AI artifacts?
The squint testBlurred down, does the composition still read?

💡 Get outside eyes, and Content Credentials

Show it to one honest person and ask "what is the first thing you notice, and is anything confusing?" And if your piece uses generative content, remember Adobe's Content Credentials can travel with the file; disclose AI edits where honesty matters (Module 6).

🎓 Course Wrap-Up

🎉 Look What You Can Do Now

Trace the whole arc. You started by fixing and exporting one photo. Now you can:

  • Work non-destructively with layers, masks, adjustment layers, and Smart Objects
  • Make clean selections and believable cutouts
  • Grade light and color with Camera Raw and Curves
  • Retouch a portrait naturally with frequency separation and dodge & burn
  • Build a composite that looks like a single photograph
  • Design with type, shapes, and layer styles
  • Use Generative Fill and Expand, and automate with Actions and batches
  • Plan, build, and present a self-directed portfolio piece

When you started Module 0, that was a wish list. Now it is a description of your skills.

🧠 The One Idea to Keep

If the tools fade from memory, keep this: work non-destructively, and decide what the viewer should see first. Nearly everything in this course is a variation on those two ideas. They will outlast every version update.

❓ A Last Common Question

How do I keep improving now that the course is over?

Make things, regularly and on purpose. Copy work you admire to learn it, then make your own. Keep the journal going. Use the Recipes when you get stuck. Skill is just reps plus reflection, and you already know how to do both.
